Using the project brief contained in `brief.md`, generate a set of open-ended initial project questions for the discovery phase of a digital product design project.

Use the following framework to guide the questions:

**Who**  
Who is the actual user? Do not rely only on a generic persona archetype. Try to identify specific roles, contexts, behaviours and constraints within the broader group of commuters. Consider differences such as travel frequency, familiarity with the route, time pressure, mobility options available, and the need to combine different transport modes.

**What**  
What problems, frictions, gaps or contradictions exist in the user's experience? Do not assume a feature or solution. Explore the whole commuting journey, especially:
- how people plan a trip before leaving;
- how they compare and combine different mobility options;
- what information they need before and during the journey;
- where information is fragmented, unclear, inconsistent or difficult to compare;
- what problems arise during the trip;
- how people react when plans need to change;
- what workarounds, tools, apps or services they currently use.

**Why now**  
Why do these problems still exist despite the number of transport, navigation and mobility services already available? Explore what current tools fail to support, where their limitations are, and whether the problem comes from fragmented information, lack of integration, trust, timing, context, personal preferences or other factors.

**What success**  
Explore what a successful outcome could look like for the user. Focus on one or more specific use-case scenarios in which a commuter is able to plan or adapt a journey with less friction, uncertainty or cognitive effort. Do not define the final solution yet.

The questions should:
- investigate real user behaviours, needs, motivations and pain points;
- cover both journey planning and problem solving during the journey;
- explore multimodal travel rather than focusing only on public transport;
- investigate how commuters currently choose between public transport, private vehicles and other mobility services;
- explore what information, tools and services they currently use and how they move between them;
- identify gaps and limitations in existing mobility and navigation services;
- help identify which problems are most relevant and worth investigating further;
- remain open enough for the research to reveal unexpected problems or opportunities;
- avoid assuming a specific product or feature;
- avoid assuming that AI is necessarily the answer.

Generate a concise but sufficiently broad set of initial questions that can guide desk research, user interviews and the early discovery phase of the project.

Save the generated questions in a Markdown file named `initial_questions.md`, divided into categories.
