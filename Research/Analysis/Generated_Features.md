# Features Proposed from the Touchpoints

The features a service could offer at each moment of the commute in [Generated_Touchpoints.md](Generated_Touchpoints.md). Every feature answers at least one touchpoint and quotes the note the need comes from. Nothing comes from competitors or from what apps usually offer. This is a proposal: what to build is Marco's decision.

- **[strong] or [weak]:** the mark follows the evidence behind the touchpoint, not the appeal of the feature. A touchpoint is strong when its graded desk notes reach `medium` or `high` under section 12 of `../desk_research_instructions.md` (one grade A note, two grade B notes that agree, or ten grade C items). Interview and survey notes are not graded yet (`grade: n/a`), so on their own they leave a touchpoint weak. A feature takes the mark of the strongest touchpoint it answers.
- **The mark measures the moment, not the wish.** Some strong features (F-032, F-035, F-053) rest on a well-documented moment, while the need they answer is voiced by one or two people. The evidence column shows which.
- **What it does** is one line of what, not how.
- **[value not traced]:** no outcome the person seeks could be traced in the notes when the user stories were written; the feature has no story. The reasons are in [Generated_User_Stories.md](Generated_User_Stories.md).
- Quotes are as written in the notes, shortened with "…". The notes behind the strong touchpoints were reopened and the quotes found in them.

## Contradictions that touch the features

The features are kept; the conflict is part of the evidence. Numbers refer to the contradictions in [Generated_Touchpoints.md](Generated_Touchpoints.md).

- **Alerts (F-020 to F-024).** Some commuters rely on alerts and others say no timely alert exists (contradiction 1). In one sample the same app sends too many and none (contradiction 4). 8 of 17 Belgian respondents do not want alerts on their usual trip ([survey_results](../Survey/survey_results.md), Q14).
- **Which source to trust (F-028, F-029).** A third-party app is "more truthful" for one interviewee; the operator's app is believed most often by Belgian respondents (contradiction 2).
- **Checking before leaving (F-020, F-025).** None of the three interviewees checks on a normal day; 9 of 17 Belgian respondents always do (contradiction 5).
- **Knowing before the station (F-020, F-064).** For planned works, knowing before reaching the station "does not seem to have a large impact" in [AP-028](../Desk_Research/Doc_Notes/Academic_Papers/AP-028_shires_2018_impact-planned-disruptions-rail.md); a simulation of an unplanned blockage gives a large saving (contradiction 6).

## Decisions taken

- **The strength rule.** Marco asked for the mark to follow the confidence of the touchpoint's evidence. The touchpoints file gives no confidence per row, so the rule of section 12 was applied to the grades of the notes each touchpoint cites. Set aside: counting interviews and the survey towards the rating, because their grading is not yet decided.
- **One feature per need.** When several touchpoints show the same need, they share one feature; a touchpoint that shows two needs has two features.
- **`related` points only to the touchpoints file.** The features are drawn from the touchpoints, and the notes are reached through them; the notes were not given a second back-link.
