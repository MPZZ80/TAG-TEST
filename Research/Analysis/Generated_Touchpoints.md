# Touchpoints of the Commute

Every moment of the journey found in the research notes, from the first thought about travelling to the arrival, classified as direct, indirect or missed interactions. Built with the prompt in [Generated_Touchpoint_Prompt.md](Generated_Touchpoint_Prompt.md). One row per touchpoint; the same moment found in several notes is one row with several citations.

- **Direct:** contact with a mobility service (app, board, train, bus, staff, parking, taxi, ticket).
- **Indirect:** a moment around the journey that shapes it without touching a service.
- **Missed:** a moment where the person could have used a service and did not; the citation shows the omission and, where the note gives it, why.
- **Quotes** are taken from the notes as written, shortened with "…". Interview quotes in the notes come from the automatic transcripts; one was checked in the transcript and is marked. Survey counts are of the 17 Belgian answers in the first export.
- Nothing here is a finding about how often a moment happens: a row can rest on one person.

## Contradictions

Both rows are kept; the conflict is a finding.

1. **Whether a timely alert on one's own train exists.** In [Marco_Palermo_notes](../Interviews/Marco_Palermo_notes.md) "There is no timely way to learn of delays and cancellations" (TP-029); in [Matteo_Rizzetto_notes](../Interviews/Matteo_Rizzetto_notes.md) a friend receives alerts from the operator's app timed for his drive to the station, and a weekly traveller in [OR-013](../Desk_Research/Doc_Notes/Online_Reviews/OR-013_apple-app-store_2026_sncf-connect-fr.md) relies on them (TP-026). They may concern different operators.
2. **Which channel to trust about a train.** One interviewee finds a third-party app "more truthful" than the operator and the boards (TP-032); among Belgian respondents the operator's app is the source believed most often (5 of 17) (TP-077); in [OF-001](../Desk_Research/Doc_Notes/Online_Forums/OF-001_ferrovie-it-forum_2019_ferrovie-it-trenitalia-sues-delay-app.md) a forum user calls the incumbent's train-status service "eccellente dal punto di vista dei contenuti" (excellent in content).
3. **The same app, warned early and not warned at all.** In [OR-008](../Desk_Research/Doc_Notes/Online_Reviews/OR-008_apple-app-store_2026_ns-app-nl.md) one traveller gets a delay message "meestal nog eerder dan via de stationsmededelingen" (usually earlier than the station announcements), another reads in the app that trains run "maar die rijden dus niet" (but they do not run) (TP-069).
4. **Too many notifications and none.** In [OR-009](../Desk_Research/Doc_Notes/Online_Reviews/OR-009_apple-app-store_2026_trenitalia-app-it.md) one commuter turns notifications off because 8-10 identical ones arrive every morning, another set them for the usual train and they never arrive (TP-028, TP-027).
5. **Checking before leaving.** None of the three interviewees checks on a normal day (TP-024); 9 of 17 Belgian respondents always check (TP-023).
6. **Knowing before reaching the station.** In [AP-028](../Desk_Research/Doc_Notes/Academic_Papers/AP-028_shires_2018_impact-planned-disruptions-rail.md) awareness before arriving at the station "does not seem to have a large impact" on what rail travellers do about planned works; in [AP-009](../Desk_Research/Doc_Notes/Academic_Papers/AP-009_leng_2020_role-information-availability-passengers.md) a simulation gives a large time saving when passengers are told at the start of an unplanned blockage. The situations differ (planned against unplanned; observed against simulated).

## Coverage gaps

Named, not filled.

- **Phases.** The last mile has 6 touchpoints, against 19 for disruptions and 21 for planning. The return trip is almost absent: only one interview mentions it, and no row is about the evening journey home except the last service of the night.
- **Modes.** Carpooling has no touchpoint at all. Taxi, ride-hailing and shared vehicles ("other mobility service", 3 rows) rest mostly on one Korean review sample. Park and ride appears only as a fallback that two interviewees name and one has never used. Motorbikes have none; a survey respondent noticed the option was missing. The car as the main leg rests on one review sample, one interview and one survey answer.
- **Observation.** There are no observation notes in the base: every touchpoint comes from what people said or wrote.
- **Contexts over-represented:** Italy in the interviews (Lombardy, Veneto and the Swiss border near Lugano, all three participants colleagues of the interviewer); Belgium in the survey (17 of 18 kept answers, French-speaking, recruited through one users' association); rail in general.
- **Contexts under-represented:** France, Spain and Germany have person-level evidence only from review samples and one forum thread; Japan only from workshops in small towns; the United States from one review sample and two old or statistical sources; rural commuters and car commuters throughout.
