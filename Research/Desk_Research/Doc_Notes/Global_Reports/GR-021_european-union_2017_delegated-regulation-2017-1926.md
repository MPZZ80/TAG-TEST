## What it says

Requires transport authorities and operators to make travel data available through national access points, in a standard format, so that multimodal travel information services can be built.

## Data and quotes used

- Each Member State must set up a national access point, a single point of access to at least the static travel and traffic data and historic traffic data of the different modes (Article 3).
- In the 2017 text, real-time data are optional: the rules on dynamic data apply "where the Member States decide to provide the dynamic travel and traffic data" through the access point (Article 5(1)).
- The dynamic data listed are exactly what a commuter needs in a disruption: disruptions for all modes, real-time status with delays, cancellations and monitoring of guaranteed connections, status of lifts and entrances, estimated departure and arrival times, availability of car sharing, bike sharing and parking spaces (Annex, point 2).
- The static data include default transfer times between modes at interchanges, planned interchanges between guaranteed services, park and ride stops, bike sharing and car sharing stations, secure bike parking, where and how to buy tickets, fares and season passes (Annex, point 1).
- When information services are linked, routing results must include, in case of disturbances, alternative travel options with times and connections, 'where available' (Article 7(2)(d)).
- Data must be reused neutrally: ranking criteria for travel options must be transparent and not based on commercial considerations, and the first itinerary shown must not mislead the user (Article 8(2)).
- Deadlines for static data ran from 1 December 2019 to 1 December 2023 for the whole network (Article 4(3)).
- The regulation was amended by Regulation 2024/490 from 4 March 2024, which replaced most articles and the annex (list of amendments on the page).

## Limits

The text read is the original 2017 version. It was largely replaced in March 2024 by Regulation 2024/490, whose text was not captured: statements on what is optional may no longer hold and must be checked on the consolidated version before use. A regulation says what must be made available, not what is in fact available or of what quality.
