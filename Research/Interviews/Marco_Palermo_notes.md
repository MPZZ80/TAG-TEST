## Who

- Lives in Verona and works with an office in Milan. He has a home in Milan, in the north-east of the city, which he uses when it is not rented out.
- He has travelled on this route for about twenty years.
- Before, he lived just outside Milan and commuted inside the urban area by underground and suburban train.
- Arriving late is not a problem in his work.

## The journey: Verona to Milan

He travels the evening before the day in the office, so as not to get up at six and not to depend on a morning train.

| Leg | What he said |
| --- | --- |
| Home to the Verona station | By car, driven by his father, who is retired. 15 minutes of driving. Sometimes by bus, and then he leaves earlier |
| Verona to Milano Centrale | Direct high-speed train at 17:12, booked with a carnet. Arrival shown as 18:25, lately 18:27 |
| Centrale to home in Milan | Underground, then the last stretch on foot |
| Whole journey | He leaves at 16:45 and is at home at about 19:00: two hours and a quarter |

- The buffer at the start: they leave 25 to 30 minutes before the train for a 15-minute drive. If they leave five minutes later he is still calm. He usually arrives when the train before his is leaving.
- The return: in the middle of the week the 17:35 from Centrale, decided the day before; or on Saturday in the early afternoon, to take it slowly. Someone fetches him at the Verona station.
- On the train he works, reads or does nothing.

## Planning

- He knows two or three days ahead, sometimes a week, at what time he will leave, because he has to ask his father for the lift.
- He books three or four days before, since it has happened that the train he wanted was sold out.
- Tools: the operator's app, and a ticket seller's app as backup "che magari mi fa vedere tutto quanto, tutte le opzioni possibili" (EN: which shows me everything, all the possible options) when his operator has no train at the time he wants.
- He calls these "flussi di esperienza così scontati" (EN: flows so taken for granted) that there is little to say about them.
- What annoys him in this phase: no seats on the train he had in mind, and the price when booking late. The carnet avoids the second.
- The ticket can be moved in the app, even five minutes before departure, while he is still in the underground: "riesco sempre a spostarlo" (EN: I always manage to move it), sometimes to another day.
- When works on the line sent the train by a longer route, three hours in all, the operator showed the longer time when he bought the ticket. He does not count that as a delay: "una volta che pianifichi, prendi e sai già" (EN: once you plan, you take it and you already know).

## How he finds out about delays

- He checks nothing before leaving. "In realtà, di eventuali ritardi lo so quando arrivo in stazione. Non controllo niente, non so niente" (EN: I learn of any delay when I arrive at the station; I check nothing, I know nothing).
- He assumes the train is late: "io do sempre per scontato che [è] in ritardo il treno", by 5 to 15 minutes.
- At the station he has his place on the platform, in front of the monitor that shows the delays.
- His train runs ten minutes behind another operator's train on the same track, so it inherits that train's delays.
- On board he does check how the train is doing, more on the way back, because someone is coming to fetch him; on the way out, to tell the friends who are waiting for him for the evening.
- Strikes reach him through the news feed on his phone, which shows him the notice. Then he decides not to go, or to go earlier.
- He never followed the notices of the regional rail operator or of the city transport company.

## When something goes wrong

- A person hit on the line: the operator took passengers by bus to an intermediate city and from there by train. He learned it on the spot, but a backup was offered at once, and he took it.
- A cancelled train: he waited for the next one, or decided not to go.
- A day of bad weather: he moved to an earlier train and told his colleagues, since he works on the train anyway.
- In his years inside the urban area, with underground and suburban train: no planning at all. If the underground was closed he found out there and worked it out: on foot or by bus to the next station, or surface lines when the suburban train was not running.
- Two early-morning trips to another city for work: the train was late both times, then a taxi, with colleagues.

## Why the train

- Driving to Milan is a burden and he does not much like driving.
- The train takes the same time or less.
- He can sit "senza ansie" (EN: without anxiety) and work or read, which he cannot do while driving.
- In Milan a car is a problem: when he had one there he did everything not to use it, so as not to lose the parking space, and after some years he took it back to Verona.

## What he would change

- Timetables should promise less: "dovrebbero essere molto più laschi con il tempo stimato" (EN: they should be much looser with the estimated time). If the 18:27 were shown as 18:40 nothing would change for him and the train would arrive on time or early, as happens with flights. It is a matter of perception: for twenty years his father, who fetches him, has said the trains are always late.
- There is no timely way to learn of delays and cancellations: "non esistono modalità di aggiornamento su ritardi, soppressioni eccetera che sia[no] effettivamente … tempestiv[e]".
- To know, he would have to go and look himself, on the operator's site or on the train-status service, and check whether the train left late from its origin, since that delay carries through the whole run. "Quella roba lì, se non te la vai a vedere tu, non te la dice … nessuno" (EN: if you do not go and look for it yourself, nobody tells you).
- He adds that he may be "uno sprovveduto che non controlla [i] ritardi" (EN: a careless one who does not check delays).

## What this adds to the desk research (agent's reading)

Finding numbers refer to `../desk_research_partial_analysis.md`. The participant knows these findings, so agreement with them proves little.

- **He protects himself with time, not with information (F72, F9).** He travels the evening before, leaves home with a wide margin and keeps the ticket movable. With these in place a delay of 10 or 20 minutes "non mi cambia" anything, and he does not check.
- **The first leg depends on another person (F74).** The lift from his father fixes the departure time days ahead and is the reason the trip is planned early. On the return, the person waiting at the station is the reason he follows the train.
- **Who is involved in the decisions (Q7):** his father for the lift, the friends waiting in the evening, colleagues to be told. The desk research had almost nothing on this.
- **He decides without a tool and checks at the station (F44, F11).**
- **A delay known in advance is not felt as a delay (F32).** The longer route shown at purchase, and his proposal of looser timetables, both say that what counts is the promise kept.
- **Flexible tickets are what he values in the tools (F24, F41).** The carnet and the change at the last minute.
- **The last leg is short and can be walked (F53).** Unlike the cases in the desk research, the long leg here is the train.
- **Nothing in this account points to scattered information as a problem.** He uses two apps and finds them obvious. What he misses is being told in time (F43).

## Not covered
