**Confidence: low.** One participant, about 22 minutes in all, automatic transcripts, and a colleague of the interviewer. Her commute is from a town on the outskirts into Milan, about once a week, so it is closer to a suburban commute than to one between two cities.

## Who

- A colleague of the interviewer. She commuted in the past to go to university and now goes to the office "qualche volta" (EN: sometimes).
- The day is flexible, but she keeps Wednesday for the office. Going to the office is her own choice: she is not obliged to.
- She lives in a town on the outskirts, where there is no shared micromobility.

## The journey, door to door

| Leg | What she said |
| --- | --- |
| Home to the nearest station | By bicycle in good weather, by car when it is cold. By car about 5 minutes with the traffic lights; she parks near the station |
| Wait at the station | About 10 minutes: she leaves home at 8:05 for the train at 8:27 |
| Train | A suburban line through the city, about 35 minutes, to a station in the centre |
| Underground | The first train that comes, to the stop near the office |
| Last stretch | On foot |
| Whole journey | From 8:05 to between 9:05 and 9:15: "direi un'ora e un quarto in tutto" (EN: I would say an hour and a quarter in all) |

- She plans backwards from the arrival: "io faccio un reverse engineering", to be in the office by 9:15 or 9:30 at the latest. She counts 45 minutes from the train to the office door.
- She leaves early on purpose: "mi piace fare le cose un attimo con calma, quindi piuttosto … esco un po' prima perché se no mi agito" (EN: I like to do things calmly, so I would rather leave a little earlier, otherwise I get agitated).

## Alternatives on each leg

- **Home to the station.** Car, bicycle or on foot. On foot it is half an hour and she has never done it: "non ho voglia la mattina di farmi mezz'ora a piedi" (EN: I do not feel like walking half an hour in the morning). The choice between bicycle and car follows the season and the light.
- **Public transport to the station is not an option.** There is a bus but on another route; she has never looked at it: "ci metto di meno [ad] andare a piedi" (EN: it takes me less to walk).
- **Shared scooters or bicycles** do not exist where she lives.
- **Train and underground.** One alternative, which she takes only when her train is cancelled: another line to a different terminus and from there the underground back up. It takes about the same time but "è stra scomodo perché su quel treno c'è tantissima gente, quindi non si sta mai seduti" (EN: it is very uncomfortable because that train is packed, you never get a seat). She calls it a cattle truck.

## Which leg is the uncertain one

- The first and the last leg are in her control: bicycle or car at one end, on foot at the other. She depends on no timetable there.
- The underground is not a worry unless there is a strike.
- The train is the complicated leg. If she misses her train she has to wait a quarter of an hour and take the crowded one.

## Tickets

- Single tickets until a few months ago; since then a carnet of ten tickets, which covers five return trips, a little more than a month for her.
- The carnet is in the regional rail operator's app. She activates one ticket when she travels: "non c'è il rischio che perdi un viaggio" (EN: there is no risk of losing a trip).
- She did not know that a carnet can expire.
- Her reason for the carnet is the price: her town is one stop beyond the point where the fare goes up, "per una stazione io pago di più il biglietto" (EN: for one station I pay more for the ticket), and with ten tickets one or two are free.

## Planning

- Very short. "Ormai conosco gli orari e so esattamente quando devo uscire" (EN: by now I know the times and I know exactly when I have to leave).
- "Il mio momento di pianificazione: prima di andare a letto mi metto la sveglia" (EN: my planning moment: before going to bed I set the alarm) for the time that lets her leave at 8:05.

## When the train is cancelled

- She finds out at the station. "Mi lascio guidare dagli eventi, arrivo lì e vedo se è soppresso o meno" (EN: I let events guide me, I arrive and see whether it is cancelled or not).
- A few times she knew before, because she opened the operator's app on a feeling: "avevo questo sentore, sesto senso, che forse dove[vo] guardare" (EN: I had this hunch, a sixth sense, that maybe I should look).
- She can afford it: "non è che devo arrivare in ufficio ad un orario preciso perché devo timbrare qualche cartellino. Se fosse una situazione diversa, ovviamente la mia tollerabilità a queste cose sarebbe molto inferiore" (EN: I do not have to be in the office at a precise time to clock in; if it were different, my tolerance for these things would be much lower).
- Her order of fallbacks:
  1. The other train from the same station, a quarter of an hour later, to a different terminus and then the underground.
  2. If that is cancelled too, she goes home. It has happened.
  3. Only for something that cannot be missed, such as an important presentation: drive to the park and ride at the end of an underground line and take two underground lines. "Ma non mi è mai capitato" (EN: but it has never happened to me).
- Other alternatives exist but are "talmente scomode che non le ho mai prese in considerazione" (EN: so inconvenient that I never considered them).
- If the underground is not running: "non vado al lavoro" (EN: I do not go to work). In other situations in Milan she walks if it is a few kilometres, has used a shared e-bike a few times at night, never a scooter because they frighten her, and for places off the underground follows the map app: "qualsiasi roba mi dica di prendere" (EN: whatever it tells me to take).

## How the information reaches her

- The display at the station.
- The operator's app, rarely and on a hunch.
- Her brother. He takes the same line, more often and earlier in the morning, and writes to her when the line is not working. "Ho la notizia, diciamo, in anteprima" (EN: I get the news, let us say, in preview). It has happened a few times, for faults on the line and not for single trains.
- The map app does not help on a route she knows: "Google Maps non è aggiornato [sui] problemi della linea" (EN: it is not up to date on the problems of the line).

## What she would add

- The trains at that hour carry more people than they can hold. She calls it a systemic problem, of infrastructure, and not one of organisation: more runs in the morning, or longer trains.

## Findings (agent's reading)

One interview confirms nothing; it shows where to look.

- **Presence in the middle of the week:** one day, usually Wednesday.
- **She combines three means and a walk without calling it a combination.** Bicycle or car, train, underground, on foot.
- **The legs in her own control are not a concern; the service with a timetable is.** The first leg is the one with real alternatives, it changes with the season, and public transport is not among them.
- **The buffer is ten minutes at the station, kept for calm and not for a risk.**
- **Comfort decides between two routes of the same length.** A seat against a crowded train.
- **No planning on a known trip, and no check before leaving.** The whole plan is an alarm clock.
- **The fallbacks are fixed in advance and ranked, and the last one is not to go.** For a hybrid worker who chooses her office day, staying at home is the cheapest fallback. The desk research counts people who leave rail in a disruption but not those who cancel the trip because they can work from home.
- **Tolerance depends on the obligation to arrive.** She says so herself. The same holds in the other interviews of this round and should be tested with someone who has fixed hours.
- **A relative on the same line is her early-warning system (Q7, Q24).** An informal channel that works because he travels earlier.
- **The carnet fits one day a week and was chosen for the price.** She did not know its expiry.
- **Against the brief:** she names crowding as the problem, a matter of service, and no problem of information or of moving between tools.

## Not covered

- What she does on the train, and the return trip.
- Delays, as distinct from cancellations.
- Refunds.
- Whether she would go to the office more often if the trip were different.

## Doubts in the transcripts

- "Rovereto": read as the underground stop near the office.
- "Porta Venezia", "Cadorna", "Rogoredo": stations in Milan. She uses "il Rogoredo" and "il Cadorna" as the names of the two train services from her station, after their destinations. In one passage of part 2 the interviewer and she mix the two names up and correct themselves.
- "Comasina", "ad uomo" (Duomo): the park and ride at the end of the yellow underground line and the interchange in the centre.
- "Come miuto", "comiuto": "commuto", she commutes. "Carne", "carné": the carnet of ten tickets.
- "Delle 27", "delle 47": her train at 8:27 and a later one at 8:47; elsewhere she says the other train is a quarter of an hour later. Check with the recording.
- The exact fares ("2 euro e 20", "2 euro e 40") are uncertain in her own words.
- The last lines of part 2 are a joke between colleagues about a reward and were left out.
