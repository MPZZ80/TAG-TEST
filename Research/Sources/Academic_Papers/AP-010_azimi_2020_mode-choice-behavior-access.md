## What it says

A model of how bus and commuter-rail passengers in the Orlando area reach the stop and leave it, built on an on-board survey of 13,181 responses in 2017. Nine in ten walk at both ends. The car is used almost only from home to the stop, in the morning peak, by higher-income passengers making a return trip; at the other end it is hardly available. Taxi and ride-hailing are 0.3% to 0.5% of connections and are used mostly for airports. The more changes a journey has, the less likely any means other than walking is used to reach it.

## Data and quotes used

- Method: on-board survey of the LYNX network, Orlando metropolitan area, January to April 2017; 13,181 responses weighted to 91,787 trips; two multinomial logit models, one for the trip to the first stop and one for the trip from the last stop, with walking as the reference; land-use data by census block group (PDF p.4, p.8, p.11).
- How people reach the first stop: walk 90.5%, dropped off, carpool or shuttle 4.9%, bicycle, scooter or similar 2.2%, drive alone and park 1.9%, taxi or ride-hailing 0.3%, wheelchair 0.1% (table 2, PDF p.6).
- How people leave the last stop: walk 92.4%, picked up, carpool or shuttle 3.1%, bicycle or similar 2.6%, drive alone 1.3%, taxi or ride-hailing 0.5% (table 2, PDF p.6).
- The car belongs to the home end: 90% of drive-alone trips to a stop start from home and 52% happen in the morning peak; 72% are part of a return trip; 80% of drive-alone trips from a stop end at home and 70% happen in the evening peak (table 4, PDF p.7). The authors: drivers 'can park their cars in or near the transit station, and pick up their cars on the way back' (PDF p.13).
- Who drives to the stop: 46% of those who drive alone to a stop have a high or very high household income, against 8% of those who walk (table 4, PDF p.7). In the model a very high income raises the odds of driving alone (coefficient 2.64) and of being driven (4.72) (table 6, PDF p.12).
- Distance: the farther the stop from the origin or the destination, the more likely any means other than walking; at the destination end the coefficients for distance are about 1.9 for carpool, taxi and driving (tables 6 and 7, PDF p.12, p.16).
- Changes: each transfer in the journey lowers the odds of reaching it by any means other than walking (coefficients from -0.17 to -0.47 at the origin end; -1.33 to -2.04 for driving and taxi at the destination end). The authors read this as people who 'might not have the option to use other modes for cost considerations or other reasons' (tables 6 and 7; PDF p.13).
- Taxi and ride-hailing as a connection: used above all to and from airports (coefficient 4.51 for trips to the airport); visitors and evening trips use them less; most users have no trip in the opposite direction (67% and 72%) (table 4 and table 6, PDF p.7, p.12-13).
- Who they are: passengers of this network are mostly low or middle income (35.4% and 40.8%); high and very high income together are 9.4% (table 3, PDF p.6).
- From the literature it reviews: a combined bus and ride-hailing service at a US university put passengers off 'mainly because of transfers and additional pickups'; park and ride at stations raised ridership in a simulation; ride-hailing raised public transport use in large US cities and lowered it in small ones (PDF p.3).
- Lack of parking and worries about security lower the use of cars and bicycles to reach a station, according to earlier studies cited (PDF p.2-3).

## Limits

One US metropolitan area with a bus network used mostly by people on low and middle incomes and a single commuter-rail line; not intercity, and not comparable with European rail commuting. The survey is of people already on board, so it says nothing about those who gave up because they could not reach a stop. Taxi and ride-hailing connections are a few hundred weighted trips, so the coefficients for them rest on very few people. Data from 2017. The paper sets out to find a market for ride-hailing as a connection and its recommendations follow from that aim. Nothing on information, tickets or disruption.
