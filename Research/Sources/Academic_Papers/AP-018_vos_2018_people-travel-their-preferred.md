## What it says

A survey of 1,656 residents of Ghent (Belgium) on their most recent leisure trip. About half (48.7%) did not travel with the mode they like most. Public transport is liked least: only 17.9% of public transport users prefer it. People who travel with their preferred mode are more satisfied whatever the mode; the low satisfaction of public transport users comes largely from the many who would rather use something else.

## Data and quotes used

- Method: internet survey in 2012 in Ghent (260,000 inhabitants); 27,780 invitations delivered by hand, 1,807 answers (6.5%), 1,656 used; 77% with a bachelor degree or higher. Leisure trips only; when several modes were used, respondents gave the one covering the longest distance; train users (2.8%) were removed (PDF p.7-8).
- Positive aspects (yes/no, 16 items) linked to each mode, average out of 16: cycling 8.87, walking 7.90, car 7.11, public transport 3.36 (table 2, PDF p.10).
- Public transport scores (share of respondents linking the aspect to it): flexible 0.05, time saving 0.09, feelings of freedom 0.09, relaxing 0.12, comfortable 0.14, reliable 0.15, privacy 0.02; possibility to perform activities during travel 0.27 (car 0.17); safe 0.43 (car 0.29). Car: comfortable 0.76, privacy 0.76, freedom 0.74, flexible 0.70, time saving 0.67, reliable 0.56 (table 2, PDF p.10).
- Yet 87% would like to live in a neighbourhood with good public transport services, the highest of the four modes (table 2, PDF p.10): people want the option even when they do not like using it.
- Mode used for the trip: car 53.3%, bicycle 20.4%, on foot 19.3%, bus/tram 7.1% (PDF p.9). Preferred mode: cycling 42.3%, car 23.3%, walking 17.9%, public transport 2.5%; 14.0% prefer more than one mode (PDF p.14-15).
- Consonance: 51.3% used a preferred mode. Cyclists 87.2% consonant; car users 56.4% and pedestrians 53.3% dissonant; 'Only 17.9% of public transport users has a preference for using public transport' (PDF p.15).
- Satisfaction: for car, public transport and cycling, scores are positive for consonant travellers and negative for dissonant ones. Quote: 'it is not the chosen travel mode itself that affects travel satisfaction, but whether the chosen mode is the preferred mode' (PDF p.16, 18).
- People with a preference for more than one mode are not more satisfied than the others, although they are less often dissonant (PDF p.17).
- Four reasons proposed for using a non-preferred mode: lack of skills ('Not (fully) understanding public transport routes and schedules and not knowing where and when to transfer'), lack of options, barriers, and habit (PDF p.4-5). These are proposed, not tested in the data.
- The author reads the result as a negative reinforcement loop for public transport: 'most public transport users might be forced to use public transport' (PDF p.18).

## Limits

Leisure trips inside one city, not commuting; the paper itself says leisure mode choice is more free than commute choice. Train users were excluded and only the longest leg was recorded, so combined trips are invisible. Data from 2012, cycling city, highly educated sample, 6.5% response rate. Preference is a crude measure (count of 16 yes/no items), as the author admits. Cross-section: no causal claim.
