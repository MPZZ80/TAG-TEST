## What it says

An expert scoring of 14 European journey planners, as they were in 2019, against a checklist of ten aspects in four groups: planning the route, booking and paying, the data handled, and extra information. No planner covers everything: the best gets 63 of 100 points and the average is 44. Planners of cities and regions are strong on route planning and live data and weak on tickets; planners of rail and air operators are strong on tickets and weak on the rest of the journey; planners that span countries have neither live data nor payment. The scores were given by transport experts, not by users.

## Data and quotes used

- Method: 14 web journey planners chosen because 'widespread or popular in a region' or with 'leading ideas'; ten aspects worth 10 points each, scored by 'transport experts' (number and identity not stated); five user groups (student, worker, tourist, businessman, pensioner) with weights set by 'a short survey among transport experts' and by 'engineering considerations'; shares of the groups from a Hungarian national travel survey (PDF p.4-8, p.10-11).
- Overall score out of 100: DB 63, BKK Futar (Budapest) 59, AnachB (Austria) 55, TfL (London) 52, Scotty (Austrian railways) 50, 9292 (Netherlands) 49, Utvonalterv (Hungary) 45, Bayerninfo 44, TripGo 43, Google Maps 40, Rome2Rio 36, Lufthansa 33, Eu-Spirit 32, RouteRank 22 (table 4, PDF p.10).
- The headline: 'even the best journey planner did receive only 63% of all possible points, and on average the journey planners received 44%' (PDF p.10). 'There is no single journey planner, which would reach a well elaborated status in the case of all main aspects' (PDF p.12).
- Booking and payment, out of 20: DB 16, Lufthansa 12, TfL 9, 9292 9, Scotty 9; BKK Futar 6, AnachB 6, TripGo 6; Google Maps 4; Eu-Spirit 0, Bayerninfo 0, Utvonalterv 1, Rome2Rio 1 (table 4, PDF p.10).
- Live and estimated data (planned restrictions, real-time traffic and travel information, crowd-sourced data, alternative routes), out of 10: BKK Futar 10, Eu-Spirit 8, Utvonalterv 8, TfL 7, AnachB 7, DB 6, Bayerninfo 6, Scotty 6, 9292 4, TripGo 2, Google Maps 0, Rome2Rio 0, RouteRank 0, Lufthansa 0 (table 4, PDF p.10).
- Planning options (time, duration, cost, number of changes, walking distance, preferred mode, park and ride, crowding), out of 10: AnachB 8, BKK Futar 7, TfL 7, TripGo 7, Utvonalterv 6; DB 5; Google Maps 0 (table 4, PDF p.10).
- The pattern by kind of planner: 'urban and regional journey planners perform better regarding route planning services and dynamic data, while international journey planners tend to apply real multimodal solutions and provide balanced results for all main aspects except booking and payment. Finally, journey planners of rail and airway services providers have better results for booking and payment and supplementary information' (PDF p.9-10).
- Combining modes in one trip: 7 of the 14 planners do it (BKK Futar, AnachB, Bayerninfo, Rome2Rio, TripGo, Eu-Spirit, RouteRank), 'and in most cases they only combine a few modes (e.g., public transport with bike sharing)' (PDF p.8).
- Where development went: it 'mainly focused on handled data and supplementary information, while, concerning route planning services and booking and payment, no real novel solutions were presented' (PDF p.9).
- The 'worker' group, defined as people who commute to work or school at least four times a week: 'Daily travel in the city is their usual characteristics, that is, why they prefer the route planning options using actual data. Comfort services have the lowest priority for them'. Weights given to it: route planning 0.35, data 0.30, booking and payment 0.20, extra information 0.15 (PDF p.6, table 5 p.11).
- Score for the worker group, out of 100: DB 76, BKK Futar 75, AnachB 71, TfL 68, Scotty 64, Utvonalterv 62, 9292 58, Bayerninfo 58, TripGo 53, Google Maps 48, Rome2Rio 46, Eu-Spirit 44, Lufthansa 34, RouteRank 22 (table 6, PDF p.11).
- From the literature it reviews: 'in Europe there are currently hundreds of journey planners, but truly multimodal journey planners are a rarity' (citing Borkowski); 'truly intermodal trip planners are currently not available' (citing Guo et al.); a study of door-to-door trips found that 'all trip legs need to be considered when evaluating the overall travel experience, especially trips legs involving waiting or transferring time' (citing Abenoza et al.); journey planners 'should also cover the whole travel process' and not only the search before the trip (PDF p.1, p.3-4).

## Limits

Scores given by unnamed experts ticking a list of features: nobody used the planners on a real journey and no user was asked. The user groups and their needs are the author's assumptions; the 'worker' is described as a city commuter, and intercity commuting is not considered. The checklist counts whether a feature exists, not whether the information is right or arrives in time, and it gives the same 10 points to station Wi-Fi and shops as to live data. A snapshot of 2019 websites, several since changed. A single author. The choice of the 14 planners is not systematic, and no Italian planner is included.
