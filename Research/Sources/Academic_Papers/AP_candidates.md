# Candidate Sources: Academic Papers

28 peer-reviewed papers, ordered by the provisional score. They cover mode choice and satisfaction, how people use information and react to disruption, first and last mile and transfers, Mobility as a Service (MaaS) adoption and trials, hybrid work and travel, and one study of intercity commuters. **All 28 were kept** (Marco: "scarica le fonti", no further questions on papers), and have source files AP-001 to AP-028. Only 8 PDFs could be downloaded: the publishers' sites block automated downloads for the other 20, which are in [AP_to_obtain.md](AP_to_obtain.md).

All papers are open access according to OpenAlex. I opened each link once; the three that answered with an error code are marked. I read the titles and the OpenAlex records only, not the papers.

**Expected grade:** reliability 3 for peer-reviewed journals, plus the date points and the citation points from the rules; the range includes the method point, which is +1 if the paper states how its data was collected (likely for empirical papers, not verified). ## What this list covers and what is missing

- **Covered:** modes and satisfaction (Q6, Q13, Q34), information and disruption (Q17, Q21–Q22), first and last mile and transfers (Q15–Q16), MaaS (Q27–Q33), hybrid work (Q32).
- **Thin:** who the commuters are (Q1–Q8, only one study of intercity commuters, in China), the emotional and practical cost of disruption (Q23) and what success looks like (Q36–Q40).
- **Geography:** author countries are mostly European, with some from Australia, China and the US. Papers on Japan, Korea and Singapore are missing.

## How the search was done

OpenAlex only, with the filter: articles and reviews published since 2017, sorted by relevance, and then by citations on nine topics: multimodal commuting and mode choice, real-time information and disruption, MaaS trials and adoption, first and last mile, intercity and long-distance commuting, commuting stress and travel time, passenger response to disruption, multimodal journey planners and user needs, and hybrid work and travel demand. One query each on habit, travel information apps, the Sydney and Helsinki MaaS trials, and papers in other languages.

## Left out

- **Papers in other languages:** OpenAlex returned few relevant ones. The best match was a French study on cross-border commuters between France and Luxembourg and Switzerland (2019, 6 citations), not listed because it is weakly cited and off topic. Japanese results were short institutional notes on MaaS with no citations. Korean search returned nothing. Journals in those languages (for example J-STAGE for Japan) need their own search.
- **Papers before 2017**, and papers on topics such as autonomous vehicles, e-scooters and carsharing that came up in the searches.
- **A Singapore MaaS testbed chapter** (Springer): a book chapter, outside the filter.
