## What it says

Presents the 2024 Eurobarometer as showing a positive booking experience, and argues that the situation is improving.

## Data and quotes used

- 73% of respondents who had tried to book two or more connecting trains with different operators found the process easy; of those who do not book combined connecting trains, only 9% said the main reason was not being able to buy all tickets in one place (page text).
- 61% of travellers found it easy to book multimodal journeys combining collective modes; of those who do not book multimodal journeys, only 5% gave not being able to buy all tickets in one place as the main reason (page text).
- CER press release of 7 April 2025 on a Eurobarometer survey commissioned by the Commission's transport department (web page, captured 2026-10-06).
- 73% of surveyed citizens who had tried to book two or more connecting trains with different operators found it easy; among those who do not book combined connecting trains, only 9% gave as main reason not being able to buy all tickets in one place (web page).
- 61% of surveyed travellers find it easy to book multimodal journeys (combining different collective transport modes); among those who do not book them, only 5% gave as main reason not being able to buy all tickets in one place (web page).
- CER names price, travel time, comfort and direct connections as the factors that count for choosing sustainable travel over long distances, and says ticketing also means accurate information, customer care and passenger rights 'including journey continuation' (web page).

## Limits

Press release by the railway companies' association, which opposes new ticketing obligations: the figures are selected to support that position. The base is people who tried to book, so those who gave up before trying are not counted. Sample size, year of fieldwork and question wording are not given. Long-distance and cross-border booking, not daily commuting. The figures should be checked in the Eurobarometer report before use.
