## What it says

Reports that the transit-technology company Via acquired Citymapper, the London startup behind a popular urban journey planning app.

## Data and quotes used

- Citymapper has around 50 million users globally; financial terms were not disclosed, and Via confirmed that it is a mixture of cash and stock (article text).
- 16 March 2023: Via (US transport technology company, valued 3.5 billion dollars) bought Citymapper. Terms not disclosed; sources told TechCrunch investors mostly did not get their money back and the price was 'well below' 100 million dollars. Investors had put in about 52 million dollars plus about 8 million from UK crowdfunding (web page, captured 2026-10-07).
- Citymapper: about 50 million users (figure not updated since 2021), 108 cities; valued over 365 million dollars in 2016; operating loss 8.1 million pounds in 2021 (7.3 million the year before) on stagnant revenue (web page).
- Why it did not pay: 'Eventually companies like Google caught up and overtook Citymapper on its key USPs'; it tried its own transport service (dropped), then ads, then a paid 'Club' tier and a 'Pass' with a travel card; take-up unclear (web page).
- What the buyer wants is the data: where people travel, 'how they're trying to use the system', and afterwards 'how did that trip actually go? Was it on time? How many connections were made?'. Via also wants to let cities send updates and disruptions to riders through the app (web page).

## Limits

News article with unnamed sources on the price. About the business of a city trip planner, not about commuters between cities. No user evidence. Figures on users are the company's own and old.
