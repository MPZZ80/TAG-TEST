## What it says

Reports the launch of flexible season tickets in Great Britain.

## Data and quotes used

- The tickets allow unlimited travel between two stations on any eight days in a 28-day period, are designed to offer savings to part-time commuters travelling at peak hours for part of the week, and could be used from 28 June (article text).
- 21 June 2021, Great Britain: flexible rail season tickets launched for people who commute two or three days a week: unlimited travel between two stations on any 8 days in a 28-day period, usable from 28 June, no need to choose the days in advance, no fee to change; an online calculator helps find the cheapest option (web page, captured 2026-10-07).
- Transport Focus chief executive: 'Our research with passengers showed us there was strong demand for a new ticket that suited people who expected to commute less frequently in future' (web page).
- Criticism by a rail commentator: 'These really aren't season tickets. This is a bulk purchase of tickets': a fixed number of trips, unlike a real season ticket (web page).

## Limits

Short news item in a human-resources magazine, 2021, Great Britain. No data on how many people bought the ticket or whether it saved money. Shows that the mismatch between season tickets and hybrid work was recognised by the industry and the regulator's watchdog; it does not show how commuters decide.
