## What it says

Reports a TNO study that predicts that the next phase of MaaS will have governments, not tech startups, in the driver's seat after the bankruptcy of MaaS Global.

## Data and quotes used

- "The bankruptcy of MaaS Global in March marks the symbolic end of the first phase of the Mobility as a Service paradigm. The next one will have governments, not tech startups, firmly in the driver's seat, predicts a report by TNO, the Dutch institute for applied scientific research."
- Article of 21 May 2024 on a report by TNO (Dutch applied research institute), 'Public Mobility: The Next Evolution of MaaS' (web page, captured 2026-10-07).
- Diagnosis of 'MaaS 1.0': technology-driven and app-centric. Quotes from the report's authors: 'The perceived problem was a tech issue, and the solution was an app'; MaaS 1.0 'overpromised, and underdelivered'; 'Creating a functioning MaaS ecosystem was more complicated than people understood' (web page).
- Many pilots were tried and 'more often than not discontinued'; 'Easy, frictionless multimodal transport ... is tough to organize, even with technology, innovation and apps' (web page).
- Places said to get it right: Vienna, Vancouver, Berlin, where public authorities lead; 'one of the first hires for Jelbi (the MaaS platform in Berlin) was a relationship manager' (web page).
- What is needed according to TNO: collaboration between public and private actors, physical infrastructure, dedicated staff and steady funding, clear pricing, and policies that reduce private car use; six levers for authorities: subsidies, limited competition, a central organisation, established standards, replicable models, shared marketing to recruit riders (web page).

## Limits

Trade magazine summary of a report by an institute that also sells advice to local governments on this topic. Opinion and case reading, no data on travellers. Urban focus. The report itself was not read.
