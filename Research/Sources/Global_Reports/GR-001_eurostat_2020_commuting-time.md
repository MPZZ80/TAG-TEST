## What it says

Where employed people carry out their main job and how long they take to get from home to work, before the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Data and quotes used

- 61.3% of workers in the EU-27 travelled from home to work in less than 30 minutes in 2019 (Highlights).
- 79% of employed people in the EU-27 mainly worked at their employer's or their own premises in 2019 (Highlights).
- In 2019, 61.3% of employed people in the EU-27 travelled less than 30 minutes one way from home to work, 26.3% between 30 and 59 minutes, 8.1% 60 minutes or more, and 4.3% did not travel (web page, section on commuting time).
- The average one-way commuting time was 25 minutes in the EU and 21 minutes in Italy, among the shortest; Latvia 33, United Kingdom 30, Switzerland and Germany 26 (web page, map 1).
- Commuting time grows with education: 40.4% of workers with a high level of education travel 30 minutes or more, against 27.7% of those with a low level (web page, figure 6).
- Men commute longer than women: 36.8% of men against 31.6% of women travel 30 minutes or more; workers aged 15 to 49 commute longer than those aged 50 to 74 (web page, figure 6).
- Workers living in cities commute longer (27 minutes on average) than those in towns, suburbs and rural areas (24 minutes); 40.3% of city residents travel 30 minutes or more against about 30% elsewhere (web page, figure 8).
- Before the pandemic only 2.9% of employed people worked mainly from home (web page, figure 1).

## Limits

Data from 2019, from a one-off module that will not be repeated, so there is no view after the pandemic. Time is one way, self-reported, with no information on the means used, the distance, the number of legs or whether the trip crosses city boundaries: the 8.1% with an hour or more is the closest proxy for long commutes but is not a count of intercity commuters. The charts with country detail were not captured in the text copy.
