## What it says

The Italian transport regulator's yearly report to Parliament. Most of it is regulation of access, tolls and tenders; the parts useful here are the complaints the regulator receives from rail and bus passengers, the sanctions on operators over information and compensation owed to season-ticket holders, the punctuality figures in regional service contracts, the regulator's reading of how demand changed after the pandemic, and the European files on multimodal passenger rights and single ticketing. It holds no data on commuters' behaviour.

## Data and quotes used

- Rail passengers sent the regulator 839 complaints and 1,934 reports in the year. The main reasons: no refund after a cancellation, no answer to the first complaint, problems with delays and missed connections, no compensation for arrival delays over sixty minutes and for cancellations not announced in advance (PDF p.148).
- For buses the reports show the same pattern: no assistance on cancellation or late departure, no refund or compensation, delays and missed connections, no information before and during the trip, complaint systems that cannot be reached (PDF p.151).
- Trenord was fined twice in 2022 for not sending notice of a train's cancellation or delay to passengers who had given their contact details when buying; a further proceeding is open because it did not comply (PDF p.143 and note 311).
- Trenord still did not recognise delay compensation to holders of integrated season tickets, and did not count partial cancellations when calculating compensation; Trenitalia Tper had adopted the compensation rule for season-ticket holders but postponed actual payment indefinitely (PDF p.142-143 and note 308).
- In the nine regional rail service contracts monitored, punctuality within 5 minutes is between 89% and 97% and regularity above 97.7%; for 2024 none of the 27 contracts had published its results. In 2023 delays increased and cancellations decreased on the year before (PDF p.122, p.216).
- A train is counted late when it reaches its destination more than 5 minutes after schedule, and a cancelled train replaced by another within half an hour is not counted as cancelled (PDF p.216, notes 431-432).
- Regional rail demand had not returned to 2019 levels in 2023, unlike long distance, "anche in conseguenza delle modifiche intervenute sui comportamenti degli utenti post-pandemia, in particolare per gli utenti identificati quali commuter, in termini di frequenza settimanale degli spostamenti sistematici": because commuters changed how many days a week they travel (PDF p.214; working translation).
- The regulator lists what changed in demand: remote work and study as a structural fact, a rebalancing between occasional and systematic travel, the perception of crowding, the cost of buying cars (PDF p.91).
- Authorities that tender local transport still plan traditional services on fixed routes and timetables, with no real study of on-demand services or of integration with shared mobility (PDF p.110).
- Trips of up to 10 km are about three quarters of the total; public transport carries 8.6% of trips and private motor vehicles about 70% (PDF p.196).
- At European level a regulation on passenger rights in multimodal journeys (COM(2023)752) would for the first time give rights to passengers using at least two collective modes; the compromise text of December 2024 defines a single multimodal ticket. A regulation on single digital booking and ticketing for rail and one on multimodal digital mobility services are in preparation, with duties to share data (PDF p.36-37, p.156).
- The regulator intends to assess rules for the public use of data produced in passenger transport, MaaS applications included (PDF p.33).
- A train left before its scheduled time because of planned works, with serious inconvenience to travellers; the regulator forbade bringing departures forward at stations where passengers board (PDF p.73).
- Passengers with reduced mobility report missing information on whether platforms and trains are really accessible (PDF p.156).
- Second reading. Italian text, working translation. Who runs local public transport by road: 280 firms in urban service, 201 of them under 1 million bus-km a year, and 417 firms in suburban and interurban service, 286 of them under 1 million (Table 20, PDF p.116). 646 firms sent regulatory accounts for 2023, 573 of them under the simplified scheme for small firms (PDF p.117)
- How the service is split into contracts: local public transport by road is divided into lots that differ by region, for example Lazio 173 lots (18 planned), Sicily 83, Lombardy 67 (18 planned), Puglia 58, Sardinia 54, Basilicata 51, Abruzzo 43, Veneto 34, Molise 33, Piedmont 23; the body in charge differs by region (agency, province, municipality). Regional rail has one basin per region (Tables 8-9, PDF p.93-95)
- Regional rail: 41 service contracts, Trenitalia in almost every region together with about twenty other operators, with end dates between 2031 and 2041 (Table 17, PDF p.107-108). Road contracts were mostly extended to 31 December 2026, so few new tenders took place and the Authority's newer rules apply only in part (PDF p.108)
- Planning: the Authority asks that planning consider the whole mobility basin 'including exchange with neighbouring basins, also to develop services integrated in planning, organisation, fares and information to users', and notes a 'structural lack of data' (PDF p.90). It recommends that contracts oblige operators to hand over data on passengers and load by time band and line, 'given the structural lack of data available to the competent bodies and the frequent difficulty in obtaining service data from the incumbent' (PDF p.92)
- Bus stations: 54 fall under the Authority's rules and 33 have published the required station prospectus, which must cover accessibility, ticket sale conditions and public information, 'favouring where possible' real-time systems for departures, bays and destinations (PDF p.83-86; Part three counts 53 stations, p.194). An inspection in October 2024 at Roma Tiburtina bus station found a prospectus not matching reality, no separation of vehicle and pedestrian flows, no marked paths for reduced-mobility users and shortcomings in information on ticket sale and to the public; a sanction procedure opened in January 2025 (PDF p.87)
- Motorways, first regulation of users' rights (delibera 132/2024): users have a right to information for 'planning the trip' and 'managing unforeseen events during the route', including travel time in free flow and with roadworks, for now and for a later planned departure (PDF p.138). Because the network is run by different concessionaires, they must provide the information jointly 'so as not to leave the user the burden of knowing which concessionaire to consult' (PDF p.138-139); a single national app is foreseen (PDF p.140). The toll network has 23 concession companies; one runs almost 48% of it (PDF p.183, 186)
- Conciliation service, 3 April 2023 to 30 June 2025: 45,317 requests, 97.3% about air travel, 1.5% rail, about 0.6% sea and about 0.4% bus (PDF p.132). Rail users must first go to the operators' own joint conciliation bodies. 'For bus transport no compensation for delay is provided' (footnote 283, PDF p.132)
- Complaints to the Authority in the year: 4,163, about 94% closed because there were no grounds for a sanction; reports also rise for trips with reduced EU protection 'such as trips under 250 km' (PDF p.146). The legends of Figures 17 and 21 list refunds, delays and missed connections, no reply and cancellations among the reasons for rail and bus complaints; the values are in the image and were not read (PDF p.149, 152)
- Part one on artificial intelligence in transport says that 'numerous Italian cities are testing' Mobility as a Service solutions, with no data (PDF p.21). Mode shares, passengers 2023: private road transport is 'predominant' both for domestic and total traffic; the percentages are in Figure 31 as an image and were not read (PDF p.181). Road public transport: 31.6 billion passenger-km, 25.8 billion of them by bus (81.6%) (PDF p.194); supply is 'significantly higher than demand' in every year for urban and interurban buses (PDF p.198)
- Read and not relevant to the commute: airport charges, motorway toll systems and service areas, ports and maritime cabotage, rail access charges, taxi licences and case law, the Authority's funding, staff, organisation, data protection, IT security, and the list of decisions and opinions (PDF p.42-66, 79-83, 96-105, 118-129, 161-180, 199-244)

## Limits

An institutional report on regulation, not a study of travellers. Complaints reach the regulator only as a second step, after the operator, so they are a small and self-selected part of the problems. Punctuality is measured by train at destination, not by the passenger's whole trip. Read in full in two readings; many charts are images and their values are not in the text copy, so the mode shares (Figure 31) and the split of complaints by reason (Figures 17 and 21) could not be quoted. The figures 12.2% and 4.4% quoted by a web summary were not found anywhere in the report. The report gives two different totals for rail passengers in 2023 (1,049.3 million in the summary box at PDF p.207 and 781.5 million in the text at p.214) without explaining the difference, and two counts of bus stations (54 at p.83, 53 at p.194). The counts of firms and lots describe the supply side; the report does not say what they mean for a traveller.
