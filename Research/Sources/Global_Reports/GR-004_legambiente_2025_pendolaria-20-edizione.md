## What it says

An advocacy report on commuter rail and public transport in Italy. Half of it argues against large road and bridge projects and for urban rail investment; the other half covers funding, the supply and quality of regional rail, fleet age, the worst commuter lines with accounts collected from commuter committees, transport poverty, and proposals. It gives named commuters' stories and committee counts of cancelled trains, which no official source provides.

## Data and quotes used

- Funding for road and rail transport was about 6.2 billion euros in 2009, under 4.9 billion in 2020 and 5.345 billion in 2025 (PDF p.29, sentence cut at 5,345).
- Daily travellers on regional trains were 2,538,000 in 2024, against 2,471,000 in 2023, 2,873,000 in 2019 and 2,671,000 in 2009; the supply of service is still at the level of more than 15 years ago (PDF p.31-32).
- National funding for regional rail and bus fell 14.81% in nominal terms from 2009 to 2025 and about 35% in real terms on the report's own inflation assumption (PDF p.29).
- Regional trains in service were 2,605 in 2024 (2,790 in 2023), with an average age of 14.7 years; 39.4% are over 15 years old. Age differs widely by operator: 38 years for ARST in Sardinia, over 34 for Circumetnea, 23.9 for EAV in Campania (PDF p.34-36).
- Roma Nord-Viterbo line: the commuter committee counted 7,000 cancelled runs in 2023, over 6,500 in 2024 and more than 8,000 in the first ten months of 2025; in October 2025 alone 1,050 runs, 994 of them on the urban section, equal to 23% of the urban service (PDF p.40).
- Milano-Mortara-Alessandria line, 19,000 travellers a day: on 30 September 2025 delays reached two hours, six runs were cancelled and police were sent to the station to distribute commuters among carriages (PDF p.44).
- Commuters' accounts on the former Circumvesuviana: a student whose 6:43 train should arrive at 7:51 and in practice arrives between 8:10 and 8:20, and who takes the 6:07 on exam days; a student using a replacement bus who says "Gli orari ufficiali non corrispondono a quelli reali" (the official timetables do not match the real ones) and leaves at 7:42 to be sure; a couple who reached a hospital more than two hours late through a station without lifts (PDF p.37-38; working translation).
- Among the chronic problems listed for Piedmont: "esagerato ricorso alla cancellazione di corse talvolta senza neanche avere pronta una valida alternativa; informazione all'utenza carente in caso di problematiche". In English: excessive cancellation of runs, sometimes without a valid alternative ready, and poor information to users when problems occur (PDF p.43; working translation).
- Tickets do not transfer between services: "Se un Frecciarossa è in ritardo un viaggiatore non può scegliere di salire su un Intercity o un regionale perché i sistemi tariffari sono diversi" (if a high-speed train is late a traveller cannot choose to board an Intercity or a regional train, because the fare systems are different). In Germany, by contrast, a ticket is released when a connection is missed or the expected delay exceeds 20 minutes (PDF p.58 and the following page; working translation).
- The new interchange at Milano Rogoredo for the Mortara line "rischia di essere vanificato in assenza di biglietti e abbonamenti integrati", risks being wasted without integrated tickets and passes; in Catania the integration of operators and fares is still unresolved (PDF p.43-44).
- From a 2023 Legambiente-Ipsos survey of over 3,000 people in five cities: three in ten gave up opportunities because of travel difficulties: work 28%, study 17%, medical visits 19%, leisure 25%; 7% are in extreme mobility poverty (PDF p.50-51).
- Transport takes 10.8% of the average household budget, 297.22 euros a month (ISTAT 2024, cited at PDF p.50).
- Rail use per inhabitant in 2023: Italy 911 km, twelfth in Europe; Switzerland 2,487 km (PDF p.58).
- Extreme weather caused 229 events that interrupted rail services between 2010 and October 2025 (PDF p.24).
- The report proposes to include shared mobility in area passes so that commuters can cover the last mile with their pass (PDF p.60).

## Limits

An advocacy report: the selection of lines and stories serves its argument, the counts of cancelled runs come from commuter committees and are not audited, and its method is not stated. It covers rail commuters, many of them on suburban lines, not intercity commuters by car or by several modes. The figures that a web summary quoted for Sicily and Lombardy belong to the previous edition and are not in this one.
