## What it says

The European Commission's impact assessment for two proposed regulations of May 2026, one on multimodal booking platforms and one on rail ticketing. It documents with a representative survey and a route analysis that travellers cannot see, compare and buy all options in one place, especially rail trips with more than one operator, and explains it by the market position of incumbent rail companies that own the main ticket platforms. It concerns regional and long-distance ticket sales; local and urban transport is outside its scope.

## Data and quotes used

- Problem 1: lack of transparency, unfair conditions and uneven playing field in the online ticketing market. Problem 2: incomplete offer of rail tickets on rail platforms (PDF p.11).
- The proposals do not apply to local or urban transport services (PDF p.6, note 3): the first and last legs of a door-to-door commute stay outside the single ticket.
- Flash Eurobarometer 551 (summer 2024, 25,805 online interviews, representative of the EU aged 15 and over): 31% of respondents had never booked a journey combining modes and 36% never one combining operators in the same mode; over a third of those who had found it difficult (PDF p.13, p.96-97).
- Reasons given for never or rarely booking combined journeys: 17% could not find a suitable combination, 5% lacked information on where to search, 5% could not buy all tickets in one place; for rail journeys with several operators the figures are higher: 23%, 11% and 9% (PDF p.13, p.19).
- 76% of respondents book transport tickets online; ease of finding travel options and of booking are among the most important factors when booking (PDF p.97).
- 78% say the environmental impact of travel matters to them, but 21% adapt their journeys; 22% find it hard to change habits and 22% find it hard to find sustainable alternatives (PDF p.12, p.97).
- On 100 origin-destination routes in the EU, multimodal options existed on 76%, but 39% of possible rail or coach connections were not visible on the comparison platform used as benchmark, and rail options were entirely missing in 29% of cases; platforms that show several modes rarely combine them (PDF p.12).
- The Commission's own estimate of what better ticketing could move is small: the maximum potential increase in rail journeys is put at 1.7%, the share who knew what to buy and could not buy it in one place (PDF p.13).
- Journey planners that give information but do not sell or link to tickets, such as Google Maps and Citymapper, are not counted as multimodal digital mobility services and are outside the rules (PDF p.14).
- Incumbent rail companies' own channels hold most online sales: about 90% of visits for long-distance rail tickets in Germany go to bahn.de; SNCF Connect is estimated at 80-90% in France; Renfe's own channels at 80-90% in Spain (PDF p.18 and note 69).
- A court in Frankfurt in October 2023 forbade the DB Navigator app from presetting a 'fastest connection' filter that favoured DB's own trains over faster alternatives (PDF p.16).
- On the ÖBB website the competitor Westbahn appears only in the trip planner, without price and without the possibility to buy (PDF p.19).
- A YouGov poll for Transport & Environment (10,514 adults in seven countries, Italy included): 61% of long-distance rail users met difficulties when booking, 45.6% want to combine tickets of different operators on one website, 43% say they would travel more by rail if booking were easier (PDF p.21).
- A 2021 study by St. Pölten University: about a third of 76 participants failed to buy an international train ticket; rail bookings took 15-30 minutes against 5-10 for flights (PDF p.20).
- Two tickets for one journey mean no passenger rights if a connection is missed; the parallel revision of rail passenger rights would treat tickets bought in one transaction on one platform as a single ticket (PDF p.6, p.9, p.12).
- National access points must hold static, historic, observed and dynamic data of the different modes, but platforms that sell tickets need further data such as real-time fares (PDF p.8).
- The European passengers' federation says that access to data, including real-time information on disruptions, is what enables such services (PDF p.98).

## Limits

A document written to justify a legislative proposal: it selects evidence that supports intervention, and a first version received a negative opinion from the Commission's scrutiny board. It is about buying tickets for regional, long-distance and cross-border trips, mostly occasional ones; it says nothing on commuters, season tickets or daily repeated trips, and excludes urban transport. Much of it concerns air ticket distribution, not relevant here. Stakeholder surveys are small and not representative (73-78 respondents); only the Eurobarometer is. The chapters on policy options and impacts (PDF p.33-90) were not read.
