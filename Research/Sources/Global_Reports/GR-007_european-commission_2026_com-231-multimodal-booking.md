## What it says

The Commission's proposal of 13 May 2026 for a regulation on multimodal booking. It sets rules for platforms that show and sell tickets of two or more transport operators: neutral ranking of results, final prices always shown, emissions shown when operators provide them, fair contract terms for the largest platforms, data shared with public transport authorities. It is a proposal, not law, and it excludes urban and suburban transport.

## Data and quotes used

- An analysis of 100 representative routes in the EU found multimodal options available in 76% of cases, yet very few multimodal digital mobility services showed options in different modes and these were rarely combined (PDF p.2).
- The 2024 Eurobarometer of 26,000 EU citizens found that one third had never booked a multimodal or multi-operator journey, and that over a third of those who had encountered booking difficulties (PDF p.2).
- The regulation would not apply to transport services whose main purpose is to serve an urban centre or conurbation, or to connect it with the surrounding area (Article 2(1)(a), PDF p.15).
- A 'journey' is defined as the end-to-end carriage of passengers, which can include several legs, one or more operators and one or more modes (Article 3(4), PDF p.17).
- Platforms would have to rank results by default on stated neutral criteria: final price, travel time, emissions, departure time, connections, accessibility; paid prominence only on the user's request and labelled (Article 7(5) and 7(11), PDF p.21-22).
- Platforms must always show the final price and let users rank by emissions (Article 7(6)-(7), PDF p.21).
- Operators' own websites and apps that sell only their own products are not covered, and neither are online search engines; platforms that are small or medium enterprises are exempt (recital 9 and Article 2(2)-(3), PDF p.11, p.16).
- The regulation does not oblige platforms and operators to sign agreements; it sets fairness rules for the agreements that exist (explanatory memorandum, PDF p.4).
- Consumer satisfaction with the availability of travel information and ease of booking would be measured every two years with a Eurobarometer (PDF p.8).

## Limits

A legislative proposal at the start of its path: the final text may change or not be adopted. It regulates the sale of tickets, not real-time information or disruption handling, and leaves out urban transport and operators' own apps, which are what commuters mostly use. Articles 8 to 19 and the annexes were not read.
