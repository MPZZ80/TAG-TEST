## What it says

The yearly picture of how Italians travel, from the Audimob survey, with focus chapters on cars, public transport, sharing, road safety and policy (decarbonisation, transport poverty, digital mobility and MaaS, urban planning). Its main message is that trips are recovering but getting shorter and more local, that the car stays dominant, and that combining modes remains marginal. The survey method and sample are not described in this synthesis.

## Data and quotes used

- In the first half of 2025 weekday trips were 102.7 million, 6.4% more than in the first half of 2024 (PDF p.3, Graf. 1).
- The synthesis states that the demand for mobility is significantly influenced by the explosion of remote activities that began during Covid (PDF p.14, section on smart working).
- Weekday trips were 102.7 million in the first half of 2025, 6.4% more than a year before and within 3% of 2019, while passenger-kilometres stayed 20% below 2019: more trips, shorter ones (PDF p.3).
- Trips up to 10 km are 81.3% of all trips; medium-range trips 16.4%; trips over 50 km only 2.2% (2.6% in 2019). 73.2% of trips stay inside one municipality (PDF p.5-6).
- The average trip outside the municipality is 25.1 km and 32 minutes; the average urban trip 3.4 km and 16 minutes (PDF p.4).
- Long-distance rail grew against 2019 (high speed +2%, Intercity +20%), while regional rail lost passengers (-11% from 2019 to 2024) and regional rail supply was 9% lower in the first half of 2025 than in 2019 (PDF p.4).
- Modal shares, first half of 2025: car 60.8%, walking 20.6%, public and collective transport 8.9% (10.8% in 2019), bike and micromobility 5.2%, motorbike 4.5% (PDF p.8).
- Intermodal trips, made with a combination of motorised means, were 2.8% of motorised trips in the first half of 2025, 3.2% in 2024 and 6.5% in 2019; higher in the North-West (5.2% in 2024) and in cities over 250,000 inhabitants (5.8%) (PDF p.8-9).
- Conclusion of the report: "i comportamenti multimodali e intermodali dei cittadini restano ancora del tutto marginali: l'utente medio tende a privilegiare la monomodality, spesso per abitudine o mancanza di informazione". In English: multimodal and intermodal behaviour remains entirely marginal; the average user tends to prefer a single mode, often out of habit or lack of information (PDF p.49; working translation).
- Satisfaction on a 1-10 scale: bus and regional train around 6.2-6.4, Intercity and high-speed trains 7.2, motorbike 7.8. Share of users giving 7 to 10: regional train 52.8%, coach 50.1%, bus and tram 47.5%, long-distance train about two in three (PDF p.12).
- Intentions: 27.8% would like to use the car less and 14% more; 25.8% would like to use public transport more and 10.9% less; all these intentions are weaker than in 2019, and the report notes that intentions usually do not turn into behaviour (PDF p.13-14).
- Remote workers stabilised at just over 3.5 million (570,000 in 2019, 6.6 million in 2020). People who work from home every day fell from 24.6% of the employed in 2020 to 4% in 2024. Occasional remote workers travel as much as or more than on-site workers, so the report warns not to overestimate the effect of remote work on mobility (PDF p.14-16).
- The car is the main mode for all groups of workers: just over 65% of trips for remote workers and just over 70% for on-site workers (PDF p.16).
- Car ownership passed 70 cars per 100 inhabitants, with 41.3 million cars in 2024; cars stand still for 93-95% of the day. The average monthly cost of a car was estimated at 334 euros in 2023, 14.5% more than in 2020 (PDF p.17-18, p.33).
- Transport poverty: in the poorest municipalities public transport is under 3% of trips against 13.7% in the richest; spending on fuel and transport is higher in peripheral and rural areas, where public transport is scarce and commuters cover longer distances by car (PDF p.10-11, p.33).
- On MaaS in Italy: its implementation "is still marked by obstacles"; the MaaS for Italy listening campaign stressed the need for closer cooperation between MaaS operators and transport operators on the standardisation and quality of shared data; business models, fragmented rules and poor interoperability are named as barriers (PDF p.42; working translation).
- The report says digital innovation is advancing on the supply side (travel information, flexible services, integrated booking and payment) but that "la recezione della domanda è ancora debole", the response of demand is still weak, and that innovation concentrates in large urban areas (PDF p.48).
- Remote work, if not balanced, can reduce the demand for public transport season tickets; the Province of Bolzano gives incentives to commuters who travel at least 18 km on 120 days a year (PDF p.43).

## Limits

A synthesis: the survey method, the sample and the full tables are in the complete report, which was not obtained. It does not isolate intercity commuters: its closest measures are trips outside the municipality and trips over 50 km. The intermodality figure counts combinations of motorised means, so walking or cycling to a station may not be included. The chapters on car sales, electric cars, road safety and low-emission zones were read and hold nothing on commuting behaviour.
