## What it says

A 2019 position on how public authorities and public transport companies should respond to the growth of mobility services and MaaS.

## Data and quotes used

- The brief states the user's problem: 'for the traveller confronted with the increasing number of options, finding the best way to move around can be challenging'; MaaS is 'about offering a complete mobility solution and taking away the hassle of finding the most suitable mobility option' (PDF p.2).
- What travellers expect, according to the public transport association: trustworthiness ('MaaS should guarantee correct real time information'), simplicity (one sign-on, 'integrated information all along the trip'), impartiality, flexibility (PDF p.3).
- 'If MaaS is not responding to the travellers needs then it will not be able to attract enough users' (PDF p.3).
- Default settings matter: 'the default settings on many route planners often favours the car, which results in this being the first and most prominent result' (PDF p.8).
- 'MaaS might be a digital gateway to different mobility services but these services need to be integrated from a physical point of view to become really attractive', through interchanges and mobility hubs (PDF p.9).
- Free parking and subsidised company cars are named as incentives that keep MaaS from being attractive to car drivers (PDF p.9).
- Operators' fears about opening data: losing the customer relationship, the MaaS provider becoming gatekeeper of demand data, unfair treatment (PDF p.5).
- Not all operators have real-time and ticketing data available in the format needed (PDF p.5).

## Limits

A position paper of the public transport operators' association, dated May 2019, before the pandemic and before most MaaS pilots reported results. It contains no data: the statements on what travellers expect are assertions. Urban focus.
