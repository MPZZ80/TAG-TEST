## What it says

Explains what mobility hubs are and how to organise networks of hubs, with examples and recommendations for authorities and operators.

## Data and quotes used

- As of January 2023, more than 7,500 cars had been removed from Bremen's streets due to car-sharing (PDF p.7).
- The brief frames the trip as this project does: 'when we look at one trip, from A to B, and we look at a door-to-door experience that combines mass public transport as a backbone and the other modes as first/last mile(s), then mobility hubs function as points of interchange between modes in an intermodal trip' (PDF p.2).
- National and international hubs have as objective to 'offer seamless transfer for long-distance door-to-door mobility offers'; rural hubs to 'connect to interurban public transport'; suburban hubs first and last mile connectivity and reducing the need for a second car (PDF p.8).
- People will walk about 150 m to reach a shared scooter or bike, against up to 500 m to reach a train (PDF p.3).
- In a survey in Bremen on car-sharing hubs, the most important factors for satisfaction were vehicle availability and then the short distance to the next station; visibility of the station mattered to 24% of women against 16% of men (PDF p.3).
- 'Availability is the guarantee for reliable/plannable journeys for customers' (on Budapest's micromobility points, PDF p.12).
- Wayfinding at a hub should put the mode before the operator's brand, because operators and brands change (PDF p.10).
- A hub does not work by itself: if there is abundant car parking around it, or its services are not attractive, the shift does not happen (PDF p.9).
- The European Commission was considering requiring more than 400 urban areas to develop multimodal passenger hubs to improve first and last mile connections and long-distance connectivity (PDF p.4).
- An evaluation by the Technical University of Hamburg (2019-2020) found that neighbourhood hubs affect the choices of people living within 200 m, and that women and families value the reliability of finding a shared car or a parking space (PDF p.7).

## Limits

A policy brief of the public transport operators' association, promoting hubs. The evidence quoted comes from projects and city reports not opened here, mostly about car sharing in neighbourhoods, not about commuters changing mode on an intercity trip. Parts of three pages were read by character recognition and are incomplete.
