## What it says

The view of the national access points on how to revise the 2017 delegated regulation so that mobility data becomes interoperable across Europe.

## Data and quotes used

- NAPCORE describes its goal as improving the interoperability of mobility-related data in Europe through standard harmonisation (PDF p.2).
- The body that coordinates the national access points supports the Commission's intention to make dynamic (real-time) data mandatory, 'due to their great added value in providing efficient travel information services' (PDF p.2).
- It warns that when data are available only for parts of the network or some vehicles, traveller information 'risks being insufficient or not accurate enough to meet the traveler's expectations' and can mislead (PDF p.3).
- It names a gap: there is no agreed standard for exchanging data on shared mobility (PDF p.4).
- It warns that without further incentives the regulation may 'mainly lead to digital support for car users', because data improve for all modes including parking and taxis (PDF p.4).
- It notes that travel times for combinations of modes must be computed by the information service provider from several data holders, not supplied as data (PDF p.3).
- European data standards are usually not free of charge, which hinders the many data holders (PDF p.5).

## Limits

A position paper by the public bodies that run the access points: it argues for funding and for limiting their duties. No date on the document; written before the 2024 revision was adopted and without sight of the draft. No data on use or quality of the access points.
