## What it says

A Eurofound report of 2023 on what 'hybrid work' means and how it was put in place in the European Union during the pandemic, built from a literature review and reports by national correspondents collected between December 2021 and January 2022. It is about work organisation, not transport. For this project it gives the share of jobs that can be done remotely and shows that reduced commuting is named as a benefit of hybrid work for workers, employers and society, without measuring it.

## Data and quotes used

- Companies show a tendency to occupy office buildings in more central areas, close to employee housing, because this saves commuting time (PDF p.25).
- 37% of dependent employment in the EU can be done by telework, between 33% and 44% across Member States, with large differences between high and low earners and between white- and blue-collar workers (Sostero et al. 2020, cited at PDF p.8).
- Reduced commuting appears among the reasons why organisations adopted hybrid work, next to lower office costs and attracting staff (PDF p.29).
- 'Time and cost savings from reduced commuting' are listed among the benefits expected by individual workers; at the level of society the benefits named are fewer traffic jams, lower emissions and time saved (PDF p.36).
- A study commissioned by Greenpeace Germany in 2020 estimated that if 40% of employees worked from home two days a week, emissions from commuter traffic would fall by 18%; Eurofound's own study found the effect of telework on emissions complex and very hard to calculate (PDF p.29).
- The literature review cites a typology of mobile workers: 'pendulums' have two recurrent work locations, 'yo-yos' return to a main workplace, 'nomads' work in more than two places; the more locations and the greater the distance between them, the greater the need to coordinate (Lilischkis 2003, cited at PDF p.11).
- Vehicles such as cars, buses, trams and trains are listed among the possible workplaces in hybrid work (PDF p.12).

## Limits

Not a transport source: commuting appears in a few sentences as an expected benefit, with no data of its own. Information collected at the end of 2021, when work arrangements were still shaped by the pandemic. Figures are quoted from other works not opened here. Read by chapter: 14 of 46 pages; the chapters not read deal with definitions, national debates and company practices.
