## What it says

A yearly opinion survey by Ipsos for an assistance insurer, run online in January 2025 on samples of adults in nine European countries, Italy included. It covers which means of transport people use and for how long, the distance from home to work, days worked from home, the monthly mobility budget, what would make people use public transport and shared means more, and attitudes. Most of the report is about car ownership and electric vehicles. It gives the only Italian figures found so far on commuting distance bands and on what would move people toward public transport.

## Data and quotes used

- 86% of Europeans own at least one car (Ipsos page).
- Distance from home to work or study, employed people and students: in Italy 4% travel more than 50 km, 37% between 10 and 50 km, 46% between 1 and 10 km and 13% less than 1 km. In Europe 7% travel more than 50 km and 41% between 10 and 50 km; Belgium has 12% over 50 km and Switzerland 9% (PDF p.12).
- In Italy 41% travel more than 10 km to work or study, the lowest share of the nine countries (Europe 48%) (PDF p.12).
- What would make people use public transport more: in Italy the first answer is to improve punctuality (41%), ahead of reducing cost (38%) and improving availability (30%). In Europe and in six other countries cost comes first (44%), then punctuality (37%) and availability (35%) (PDF p.20).
- Use at least once on a weekday or weekend day: in Italy 93% use a personal car, 96% walk, 46% use public transport; in Europe 84%, 95% and 58%; in Switzerland 76% use public transport (PDF p.11).
- On a normal weekday 39% of Italians use a personal car for more than an hour a day, the highest of the nine countries (Europe 28%); 13% use public transport for more than an hour (Europe 17%, Austria 24%) (PDF p.7).
- Italy has the highest use of car sharing among the nine countries after Portugal: 27% use it at least sometimes (Europe 19%) (PDF p.11).
- Work from home: 64% of Italian workers never work from home (Europe 56%, up 8 points in a year); those who do average 0.9 days a week in Italy and 1.2 in Europe; the share with no regular days fell by 10 points in Europe, so home days became more regular (PDF p.13).
- Monthly mobility budget, insurance excluded: 122 euros on average in Italy, 159 in Europe, 278 in Switzerland; 13% of Italians spend more than 200 euros (PDF p.14).
- Among those who changed habits in five years, the reasons are cost of transport (32% in Europe, 30% in Italy), a change in family situation (30%), environmental concern (23%), moving home (21%), a new job (16%), rules imposed by local government (16%), more flexible ways of working (15%) (PDF p.19).
- Compared with five years ago, in Italy walking shows the largest net increase (+25) and public transport almost none (+2), against +11 in Europe (PDF p.18).
- Intentions for the next twelve months: 14% of Europeans mean to use the personal car more and, in the report's summary, 16% less; 25% mean to use public transport more (PDF p.24, p.30).
- Attitude and action: 72% of Italians say they pay attention to the environmental impact when choosing a means of transport and 67% say they have already changed daily habits to be greener, while 93% use a personal car (PDF p.29, p.11).
- For car sharing the levers named are lower cost (41% in Europe) and better availability (33%) (PDF p.21).
- 87% of Europeans own at least one car; one in three car owners says they would be ready to stop owning one in the future, more so in Italy, Spain and Switzerland (summary pages, PDF p.41, p.50).

## Limits

A survey paid for by an insurer that sells mobility assistance, with its own commercial reading in the foreword. Online panel, quota samples; the number of interviews per country could not be read from the page image. Stated behaviour and intentions, not measured travel. The distance question covers workers and students together and gives no means of transport by distance band, so the 4% over 50 km in Italy cannot be split by car, train or several legs. The chapters on vehicles, electric cars and micromobility were read only in their summary pages.
