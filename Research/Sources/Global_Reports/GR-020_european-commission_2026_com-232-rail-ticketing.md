## What it says

The Commission's proposal of 13 May 2026 for a regulation on rail ticketing. It would oblige rail operators to make their tickets available to online ticket sellers that ask for them, oblige incumbents with 50% or more of a national market to host competitors' tickets on their own platforms and to show all operators' timetables, and require tickets to be on sale at least five months ahead. It states the problem as the Commission sees it: buying rail tickets is too difficult when a trip uses trains of more than one operator.

## Data and quotes used

- The Commission's own statement of the problem: "For consumers, buying rail tickets is too difficult, especially when travelling cross-border or when the trip involves the use of trains from multiple operators" (PDF p.3).
- "The digital revolution in retail experienced in other sectors (hotels and air travel) has not materialised for rail"; incumbents act as gatekeepers, running most services and owning the ticket platform (PDF p.3).
- Ticket sellers would have the right to combine rail products of different operators at the consumer's request, provided minimum connecting times at the stations where trains are changed are respected (PDF p.5).
- Incumbents' platforms would have to display the full rail offer of all operators and include it in search results, using the data in the national access points, even without a request from competitors (recital 10, PDF p.14).
- The rule on national access points requires real-time data for all modes "but only for information purposes", not for selling tickets (PDF p.6).
- The 2021 rail passenger rights regulation, applied from June 2023, improves real-time travel information, adds a right to re-route oneself and requires operators under the same ownership to offer through-tickets (PDF p.6).
- Tickets should be on sale at least five months in advance (recital 9, PDF p.14).
- Scope of the rail ticketing proposal, read in the second pass: it applies to railway service providers and online ticketing service providers; it 'does not apply to: (a) metro and tram services; ... (c) railway services on standalone networks intended only for the operation of urban or suburban railway services; (d) online search engines' (Article 2, PDF p.16-17). Regional trains on the national network are inside the scope.
- Sharing obligation: a railway service provider 'shall conclude a commercial agreement with a requesting online ticketing service provider' to display, link to, resell or distribute its rail products; negotiations within two months, agreement within eight (Article 4, PDF p.18).
- Real-time data: on request the railway must supply the ticket seller with 'all the real time data for the related railway passenger services which those railway service providers make available to consumers through any of their own online interfaces', and the data must be 'accurate, complete and up-to-date' (Article 6(1)(c) and (d), PDF p.19).
- Combining and single tickets: the railway must allow the ticket seller 'to combine any rail products, including those of different railway service providers', provided the minimum connection times are applied, and 'to sell that combination as a single ticket' (Article 6(1)(e) and (f), PDF p.19-20).
- Hosting obligation: the ticket seller that belongs to a railway with 50% or more of a country's rail passenger-kilometres must sell other operators' products on request and, within 12 months, show in its search results 'all available railway services' in that country, using the timetables of the national access points (Articles 5 and 7, PDF p.18-21).
- Tickets on sale at least five months before the service runs (Article 8, PDF p.22).
- Who can complain: ticket sellers and railways, to the national rail regulator. The text gives passengers no complaint under this regulation (Articles 9 to 11, PDF p.22-24).
- Timing: applies 12 months after entry into force; the financial statement expects the start-up from 2028 to 2030 and 2 Commission posts (Article 15 and PDF p.24, p.27-29).
- The Commission's own precedent: the code of conduct for airline reservation systems of 1989, evaluated in 2020, 'has not fully ensured a level playing field', while the neutral display stayed important (PDF p.27-28).
- 'This initiative has no requirements of digital relevance ... No digital solution is introduced by this initiative' (PDF p.39-40).

## Limits

A proposal, not law; the final text may differ. It concerns the sale of rail tickets and, by its own impact assessment, not urban transport. It does not deal with season tickets, with other modes in the same trip, or with information during disruption. Only the explanatory memorandum and the first ten recitals were read; the articles were not.
