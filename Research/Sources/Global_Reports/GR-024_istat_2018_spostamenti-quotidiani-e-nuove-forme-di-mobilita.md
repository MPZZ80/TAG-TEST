## What it says

Daily travel for study or work in Italy and new forms of mobility, with times and means of transport.

## Data and quotes used

- About 30 million people travel every day to reach their place of study or work: 35.5% of the population for work, 18.5% for study (PDF p.1).
- 51.6% of employed people travel outside their own municipality and about one in three needs less than 15 minutes to reach the destination (PDF p.1).
- Trips shorter than 15 minutes fell from 49.1% in 2007 to 45.9% in 2017, and trips outside the municipality rose from 41.6% to 44.3% (PDF p.1).
- 84.6% of commuters always use one single means of transport. Using more than one means concerns 11.2% of employed people and 34.4% of students; combining public and private means concerns only 4.8% of commuters, 4.1% of the employed (PDF p.1, p.5).
- Among employed people 73.7% use only private means and 7.0% only public means (PDF p.5).
- Trips by public transport are the longest: 46 minutes on average when combined with private means, 42 minutes when public only, against 24 minutes by private means (PDF p.7).
- "Chi deve recarsi fuori dal proprio comune sceglie più spesso la multimodalità (68,7% degli spostamenti)": those who travel outside their municipality choose more than one means more often. Combined public and private use is highest in the outskirts of metropolitan areas (6.3%) and in Liguria (6.5%) (PDF p.7; working translation).
- Trips under 15 minutes fell from 49.1% to 45.9% between 2007 and 2017, trips outside the municipality rose from 41.6% to 44.3%, and trips of variable duration increased; the section is titled "Aumentano il raggio degli spostamenti e l'incertezza della durata", the range of trips and the uncertainty of their duration are growing (PDF p.1, p.3).
- 16.4% of commutes last more than 30 minutes; in Lazio 16.9% of employed people take over an hour. One commuter in four leaves home before 7:00 (PDF p.3-4).
- Commuting is among the least pleasant activities of the day, and less pleasant by public transport than by car (time-use survey 2014, cited at PDF p.8).
- Car pooling concerns 7.4% of commuters; bike sharing had about 0.5 million users and car sharing about 750,000 in 2017, concentrated in large cities of the North-West and Centre (PDF p.8-12).
- Method: the survey Aspetti della vita quotidiana 2017, about 28,000 households, first year with mixed web and paper collection (PDF p.14-15).

## Limits

Data from 2017, before the pandemic and before remote work spread. "Outside the municipality" is not the same as intercity. The share of several means counts any combination, including walking legs as defined by the survey, so it is not a measure of door-to-door intermodal trips.
