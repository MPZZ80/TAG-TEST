## What it says

Commuting behaviour in the United States in 2022, compared with 2019 and 2021.

## Data and quotes used

- 75.9% of workers drove alone to work in 2019, about 67.8% in 2021 and 68.7% in 2022 (PDF p.2).
- Average one-way travel time to work was 27.6 minutes in 2019, a 10-year low of 25.6 minutes in 2021, and 26.4 minutes in 2022 (PDF p.6).
- In the United States in 2022, 68.7% of workers drove alone to work (75.9% in 2019), 8.6% carpooled, 3.1% used public transport (5.0% in 2019, 2.5% in 2021), 2.9% walked or cycled, and 15.2% worked from home (5.7% in 2019, 17.9% in 2021) (PDF p.2, p.4).
- Average one-way travel time to work was 26.4 minutes in 2022, against a historic high of 27.6 in 2019 and 25.6 in 2021; it is longer in metropolitan areas (26.8) and longest outside their principal cities (27.8) (PDF p.6-7).
- About 9 million fewer workers left home in the core hours 6:00-8:59 in 2022 than in 2019 (83.3 against 92.1 million), a drop of about 10% that the authors link to work from home (PDF p.7-8).
- About 70% of transit commuters in metropolitan areas live in seven metro areas; in none had transit commuting returned to 2019 levels by 2022 (PDF p.3).
- The survey asks for the one means used for most of the distance on most days: people using different means on different days, or several means in one trip, are recorded under one only (PDF p.1, figure 1).

## Limits

United States, 2022. The question records one main means per worker, so trips with several legs are invisible. Travel time is self-reported, one way, door to door. Tables and charts did not convert to text: time by means of transport and the shares by length of commute were not read. No distinction between commutes inside and between cities.
