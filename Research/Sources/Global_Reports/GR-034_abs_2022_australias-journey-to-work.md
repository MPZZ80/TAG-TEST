## What it says

The methods of travel Australians used to get to work on Census day, 10 August 2021, during the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Data and quotes used

- Respondents could record up to three methods of travel to work, so combined modes are visible (article text).
- On census day in August 2021, 53.1% of employed Australians drove to work (62.0% in 2016), 21.0% worked at home (4.7% in 2016), 2.5% took the train (7.2% in 2016) and 1.8% the bus (3.5%) (web page, first table).
- Public transport commuters more than halved, from 1.25 million in 2016 to just over half a million in 2021; train commuters fell from 770,765 to 299,053 (web page, public transport).
- Outside the capital city areas driving is more common (62.9% of workers against 48.7% inside), and only one public transport commuter in ten lives outside a capital city area (web page).
- The census lets people record up to three methods of travel, but the published figures reduce them to one main method by a priority rule (web page, note to the first table).
- Work from home rose also in states with no stay-at-home orders on census day, which the statistics office reads as 'a greater societal change toward more flexible working arrangements' (web page).

## Limits

A single day, 10 August 2021, with lockdowns in the two largest states: the levels are not normal and should not be read as the present. One main method per person in the published tables, although up to three were collected. No travel time or distance on this page.
