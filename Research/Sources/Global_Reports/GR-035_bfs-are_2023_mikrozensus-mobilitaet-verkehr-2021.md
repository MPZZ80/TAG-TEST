## What it says

Switzerland's national travel survey for 2021 (55,018 people, by phone). It records every leg of a trip, so it shows how means are really combined: 82% of trips have one leg only; the common combination is walking plus public transport; combinations with car or bike plus public transport are each under 1% of trips. Rail is the long-distance means (average leg 26.8 km) and is used mainly on weekdays. More than half of adults hold a public transport pass. The year 2021 was still affected by Covid, so levels are lower than normal.

## Data and quotes used

- In 2021 leisure accounted for 43% of distance, followed by commuting with 28% (PDF p.19: "Mobilitätszweck waren 2021 mit einem Distanzanteil von 43% die Freizeitaktivitäten, gefolgt von den Arbeitswegen mit 28%"; working translation).
- Annual mobility including abroad was about 14,900 km per person, roughly 40% less than in 2015 (PDF p.19; working translation).
- Method: 55,018 people chosen at random, computer-assisted phone interviews, routes geocoded during the interview; survey moved from 2020 to 2021 because of Covid (PDF p.9-10). A leg (Etappe) is the smallest unit: at least 25 m, one means, walking included; a trip (Weg) is defined by its purpose (PDF p.25).
- Trips by number of legs, 2021: 82% one leg, 18% several legs: 'Folglich wurde das Verkehrsmittel bei weniger als einem Fuenftel der Wege gewechselt' (EN: the means was changed on less than a fifth of trips) (PDF p.26).
- Combinations as share of all trips: walking + public transport a good 9%; walking + car/motorbike 3%; triple combinations such as walking + car + public transport are each under 1% (PDF p.31-32).
- Among trips that use public transport: walking + tram/bus 51%; walking + tram/bus + train 19%; walking + train a good 17%; the rest (car or bike + train, etc.) are small shares shown only in a chart (PDF p.39).
- Access legs to public transport stops: on foot almost a third are 0.2 km or less (68% are 0.5 km or less), only 11% over 1 km; by bike 78% and by car 92% are over 1 km. Base: 30,575 walking legs, 848 bike legs, 760 car legs to or from stops, which also shows how rare bike and car access is (PDF p.39).
- Daily distance per person 30.0 km (36.8 km in 2015, minus 19%, first fall since 1994, attributed to Covid); daily travel time 80.2 minutes including waiting and changing, 74.6 without (PDF p.22, 24). Waiting and changing time is therefore about 5.6 minutes a day on average over the whole population.
- Share of daily distance: car 69% (20.8 km), train 16% (4.9 km), on foot 5%, bus and tram 3%, bike 3%. By number of legs walking is first with 42%, car 37%; by time walking 40%, car 39%, train 6% (PDF p.28). The report calls walking a 'Scharnierfunktion' (EN: hinge function) because it gives access to and change between means (PDF p.28).
- Train: average leg 26.8 km and 24.9 minutes, 61 km/h; car leg 14.8 km, 38 km/h; 69% of train legs are over 10 km against 37% of car legs; bus and tram are used 81% for legs of 5 km or less (PDF p.30-31).
- Outings (home to home) over 50 km: train is the main means for 23%; outings over 15 km: car for three quarters (PDF p.28-29).
- Train share of distance is 18% Monday to Friday against 11% Saturday and 13% Sunday (PDF p.32). Train distance per person fell from 7.5 km (2015) to 4.9 km (2021), car from 23.8 to 20.8 km; values moved back toward 2015 in the second half of 2021 (PDF p.29).
- Rail has sharper morning and evening peaks than the car; one reason given: 'Wer mit dem Zug zur Arbeit faehrt, ist an den Fahrplan gebunden und legt haeufig laengere Strecken zurueck' (EN: people who go to work by train are bound to the timetable and often travel longer distances) (PDF p.45).
- Passes: 53% of residents aged 16 or more hold a public transport pass: half-fare 35%, regional network pass 11%, general pass (GA) 9%; all lower than 2015 (PDF p.16). 93% of public transport km are made by pass holders. On an average day 41% of GA holders, 46% of network-pass holders and 15% of half-fare holders use their pass; work trips are 39% of GA distance and 40% of network-pass distance (PDF p.40).
- Long-distance trains carry 56% of rail km; regional trains (S-Bahn included) 36% of km but 66% of rail legs (PDF p.40).
- Work trips are 28% of daily distance for the whole population (8.3 km per person per day), 38% for people in work and 49% for them Monday to Friday (PDF p.48). Car occupancy on work trips is 1.09: one person in the car on 92% of legs (PDF p.37).
- Home working: workers who can do part of their work at home travel 12.8 km a day for work against 15.3 km for those who cannot; the gap is small 'auch weil Personen mit der Moeglichkeit der Heimarbeit zwar weniger, dafuer im Durchschnitt laengere Wege fuer die Arbeit zuruecklegten' (EN: because people who can work at home make fewer but on average longer work trips) (PDF p.48). Their total daily distance is the same: 37.6 against 37.2 km (PDF p.27).
- Congestion: 21% of drivers are in a traffic jam on the way to work or study at least once a week, 6% every day; it depends on where the workplace is (city or intermediate) more than on where people live (PDF p.37).
- Sharing: car-sharing membership 4.5% of licence holders (21% in households without a car, 6% in cities, 1% in rural areas); bike-sharing 1.5% of adults; 18% have ever used a ride-hailing service (PDF p.16). Scooters and similar devices: 0.1% of distance (PDF p.43).
- Car ownership: 71% of households in cities, 89% intermediate, 91% rural; 69% of licence holders always have a car available (PDF p.14-15).
- Fast e-bikes are a commuting vehicle: 65% of their distance is for work (75% among workers); average work leg 9.1 km (PDF p.42).

## Limits

Switzerland: dense, punctual public transport with integrated fares and very high pass ownership; not comparable with Italy without care. Data are from 2021, a Covid year: the survey says the fall in rail use is mainly a pandemic effect and partly reversed in the second half of the year. One-day diary. Sections on population groups, agglomerations, journeys with overnight stay and attitudes were not read. Charts did not convert, so some shares (for example car + train or bike + train among public transport trips) are known to exist but the values were not captured.
