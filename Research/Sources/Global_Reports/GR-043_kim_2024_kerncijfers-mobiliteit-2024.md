## What it says

Key figures on Dutch mobility in 2023, including the distances travelled by purpose and mode and the effect of working from home.

## Data and quotes used

- Of the distance travelled in 2023, about 25% was for commuting, and the distance for commuting and education has stayed lower since 2019 (PDF p.3: "ongeveer 25% voor woon-werkdoeleinden"; the sentence on the leisure share is cut in my extraction; working translation).
- About 43% of workers worked at home at least one hour a week in 2023, compared with about 30% in 2013 (PDF p.3: "circa 43% van de werkenden werkte in 2023 minstens 1 uur per week thuis, waar dat in 2013 nog circa 30%"; working translation).
- In the Netherlands in 2023 people travelled about 16% less distance for commuting than in 2019 (49.8 against 58.9 billion km); the institute's main explanation is more work from home and online meetings. Commuting is about 25% of all distance travelled (PDF p.3, p.19; working translation from Dutch).
- 43% of workers worked from home at least one hour a week in 2023, almost as many as in 2020-2021 (45%) and well above the roughly 30% before the pandemic (PDF p.19).
- Train use in 2023 was still 18% below 2019 (16.4 billion passenger km), and bus, tram and metro 17% below; car driver distance 8% below, mostly because of fewer commuting trips (-14%) (PDF p.20).
- The institute treats a lasting change in behaviour after the pandemic as a fact for its forecasts: work from home, online meetings and online education, and also 'a substitution from public transport to other means' (PDF p.45).
- Average delay on the main rail network was 2.0 minutes per passenger per journey in 2023, up for the second year; delay as a share of planned journey time rose 18% in a year (PDF p.32).
- Train supply and demand moved in opposite directions in 2023: 3.5% fewer train kilometres run and 16% more passenger kilometres, so trains were fuller (PDF p.32).
- Road traffic peaks on Fridays; time lost in congestion in 2023 was highest on Tuesdays (Thursdays in 2019 and 2022). Morning peak delay was close to 2019 again, the evening peak still clearly lower (PDF p.29-32).
- Dutch statistics count legs: a 'verplaatsing' is a trip with one purpose whatever the means, and a 'rit' is the part made with one means; going from home to work by bike to the station, train and then on foot to the office is one trip of three legs (PDF p.23).
- Since 2022 the national travel survey asks residents whether they can reach destinations when they want to and, if not, why (health, cost and so on), a measure of experienced accessibility (PDF p.28).
- Forecast to 2029 in the base scenario: train use +22% on 2023, bus, tram and metro +14%, road traffic +9%, cycling +11% with e-bike use +40% (PDF p.8, p.48).

## Limits

The Netherlands only: a dense country with a national rail network, a single public transport card and very high bike use to stations, so levels do not transfer to Italy. Distances by purpose come from a sample survey whose method changed in 2018; the institute notes the statistical uncertainty. Nothing is broken down by commuting distance or by number of legs. The tables of key figures did not convert and were not read.
