## What it says

Japan's yearly government survey of telework (fiscal 2024, 40,000 workers). About a quarter of employees telework, and the share rises steeply with commute time: half of those who commute an hour and a half or more, against 13.5% of those under half an hour. Rail and bus commuters telework three times as much as car commuters. Among teleworkers hybrid work has settled at about two days a week, and most would like more.

## Data and quotes used

- Method: web survey by the Japanese transport ministry with other ministries, fiscal 2024 (published March 2025); 40,000 workers: 36,219 employees and 3,781 self-employed; sample drawn to match the national employment survey by sex and age and by the three large metropolitan areas against the rest (PDF p.4, 6).
- Share of employees who telework, by one-way commute time, fiscal 2024: under 30 minutes 13.5%; 30 to 60 minutes 29.2%; 60 to 90 minutes 43.3%; 90 minutes or more 50.6%; all employees 24.6%. The survey's own summary: JA '通勤時間が長くなるほど高い' (EN: the longer the commute, the higher) (chart, PDF p.14).
- Same split before the pandemic (fiscal 2019): 9.9%, 18.2%, 24.0%, 30.8%; all 14.8%. The gap by commute time existed before and widened (chart, PDF p.14).
- By commute mode, fiscal 2024: rail and bus 42.7%; bicycle and walking 14.3%; car and motorbike 13.2%; all 24.6%. In fiscal 2019: 21.5%, 8.7%, 11.4% (chart, PDF p.15).
- All employees who telework: 14.8% (fiscal 2019), 23.0% (2020), 27.0% (2021, the peak), 26.1%, 24.8%, 24.6% (2024): a partial return, still well above before (charts, PDF p.14, 16). The survey's summary calls this a settling trend (PDF p.4).
- Employer rules: 33.1% of employees work where telework is allowed by rule or practice (40.0% at the 2021 peak); of these 63.1% have teleworked (chart, PDF p.16).
- How often teleworkers do it, fiscal 2024: 5-7 days a week 16.7%; 4 days 9.0%; 3 days 11.2%; 2 days 14.9%; 1 day 15.3%; the rest less than weekly. Average 2.1 days a week (2.4 at the 2020-21 peak; 1.9 in 2019). The survey's summary: hybrid work combining office and telework 'is settling' (chart, PDF p.19).
- Over all employees, fiscal 2024: 84.4% did not telework at all in the year; about 8% telework at least two days a week (2.6% 5-7 days, 1.4% 4 days, 1.7% 3 days, 2.3% 2 days) (chart, PDF p.20).
- Wished against actual, teleworkers who want to continue (n=5,672): actual: 5 days or more 14.4%, 4 days 8.7%, 3 days 10.7%, 2 days 14.3%, 1 day 13.5%, less than weekly 38.4%; wished: 23.4%, 10.4%, 17.4%, 24.8%, 23.9%, and nobody less than weekly. Over half wish 3 days or more; over 70% wish a hybrid with at least one day at the office (chart, PDF p.23).
- By sex and age, employees: men 31.2%, women 16.9%; men aged 30-59 about 32% (chart, PDF p.10).

## Limits

Japan: very long rail commutes into three large metropolitan areas, so levels do not transfer to Italy, though the direction (longer commute, more telework) matches Germany and Madrid. A web survey, which may over-count people at ease with digital tools. 'Teleworker' includes anyone who teleworked at all, also a few days a year. Cross-section: it cannot say whether telework lets people accept a longer commute or long commuters ask for telework. Figures were read from chart images, not from text: check them on the PDF page before citing. Less than half of the report was read. Working translation from Japanese.
