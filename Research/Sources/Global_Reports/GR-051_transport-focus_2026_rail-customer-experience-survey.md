## What it says

The first release of Britain's new official rail passenger survey: 100,982 passengers surveyed on the train between 12 October 2025 and 31 March 2026. It reports satisfaction with the journey, punctuality, frequency, value for money, delays and how they were handled, journey planning, ticket purchase, stations, trains and information, with breakdowns by journey purpose. Commuters score lower than leisure travellers on every measure reported.

## Data and quotes used

- Among passengers who were delayed on their journey, less than half were satisfied with punctuality (45 per cent) and 48 per cent were satisfied with how well the delay was handled (page text).
- Commuters were less likely to be satisfied with how the delay was handled (41 per cent) than those travelling for leisure (55 per cent) (page text).
- Overall, 87 per cent of passengers were satisfied with the train they were travelling on; satisfaction with the frequency of trains on the route was lower, at 76 per cent (page text).
- Overall journey satisfaction: 87% of passengers; 83% of commuters, 87% of business travellers, 90% of leisure travellers (section 3).
- Value for money: 59% satisfied overall, 49% of commuters against 67% of leisure travellers; 43% of delayed passengers against 63% of those not delayed (section 3).
- 17% of passengers said their journey was delayed: 62% of these by less than ten minutes, 27% by 10 to 29 minutes, 11% by 30 minutes or more (section 4).
- Journey satisfaction falls with the length of the delay: 92% not delayed, 83% under 5 minutes, 69% for 5 to 14 minutes, 54% for 15 to 29 minutes, 40% for 30 minutes or more (section 4, chart 4.3).
- Only 48% of delayed passengers were satisfied with how the delay was handled, and 41% of commuters against 55% of leisure travellers; 26% of commuters were dissatisfied (section 4).
- In a delay, passengers were least satisfied with the information about alternative routes, onward travel or connections (37%) and with how clearly the reason was explained (40%); most satisfied with how easy it was to find information (60%). Accuracy of delay information 53%, usefulness 49% (section 4).
- Before going to the station 38% looked for no information and 'just turned up'; 43% checked live departures for punctuality or platform; 35% checked scheduled times and ticket options; 3% looked up how busy the train would be (section 5).
- Passengers looked for journey information mostly on an app (68%), then Google or Apple Maps (18%); 95% found it easy to find the information they needed when planning (section 5).
- At the station 51% looked at departure boards, 25% used an app, 23% listened to announcements, 9% asked staff; on the train 36% listened to announcements, 30% used displays, 29% apps (section 9).
- Tickets: 37% bought on a mobile app, 25% tapped a contactless card or phone (32% in London and the South East, 2% on long distance); of app buyers 63% used Trainline and 20% the operator's app; 53% bought on the day of travel (section 6).
- 78% found it easy to understand the ticket types and 83% which trains the ticket was valid for (section 6).
- Satisfaction with service frequency: 76% overall, 69% of commuters; punctuality 85% overall, 80% of commuters (section 4).
- Availability of staff is the weakest rating: 72% at the station and 58% on the train (sections 7-8).
- Trust in the rail industry: 21% high, 34% medium, 45% low or none, a net score of -25; yet the net score for recommending the train is +42 (section 11).

## Limits

Statistics 'in development', first release. The sample is of journeys, recruited on board a running train, so cancelled services are not covered and delays that happen after the questionnaire is completed are missed: the survey understates disruption. It covers the rail leg and the departure station only, not the door-to-door trip. Britain only. Breakdowns by purpose beyond those quoted are in data tables that were not downloaded.
