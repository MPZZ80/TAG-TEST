## What it says

Operator performance on complaints and on delay compensation (Delay Repay) in 2024-25.

## Data and quotes used

- "Passengers are entitled to compensation from train operators if they are delayed beyond certain thresholds (known as 'Delay Repay')."
- There were 8.3 million delay compensation claims closed by train operators in 2024-2025, an increase of 9% on the previous year; and 46,329 complaints on delay compensation, an increase of 3.6% (page text).
- Only 31% of British rail passengers who complained in 2024-2025 were satisfied with how the operator handled their complaint, and 29% with the outcome, in a survey with over 23,000 responses; the regulator calls this 'too low when compared to other sectors' (web page, sections 1 and 2).
- Passengers were least satisfied with the operator seeming keen to reach an agreeable outcome (27%) and with the complaint being fully addressed (28%); most satisfied with politeness (68%) and ease of complaining (58%) (section 2).
- Punctuality and reliability is the first cause of complaint, 19.3% of the 322,409 complaints closed in the year; other common themes are room to sit or stand, on-board facilities, ticketing and refund policies, and the compensation claims process (section 2).
- 8.3 million delay compensation claims were closed in the year, 9% more than the year before; 81.6% were approved and 99.1% closed within 20 working days (section 3).
- Complaints about delay compensation were 12.7% of all rail complaints, up 1.4 points; the claims process itself was the subject of 4.5% of all complaints (section 3).
- Among disabled and older passengers dissatisfied with assistance, 66% did not complain, most often because they could see no benefit in doing so (41%) (section 2).
- Operators say they improved communication in disruption through emails about service disruption, social media and better websites (section 2).

## Limits

Britain only, rail only. Complaints and claims come from people who chose to complain or claim, so they under-count problems, as the regulator's own figure on disabled passengers shows. The satisfaction survey covers only people who complained. Nothing is broken down by commuters.
