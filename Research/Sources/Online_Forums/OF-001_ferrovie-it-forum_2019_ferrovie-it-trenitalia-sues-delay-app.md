## What it says

A thread in the Ferrovie.it forum about Trenitalia taking legal action against an app that shows train delays. Page 1 of 3 was captured.

## Data and quotes used

- Title of the thread (Italian): "Trenitalia fa causa a un'app che mostra i ritardi"; in English: Trenitalia sues an app that shows delays (working translation).
- Thread opened on 29 July 2019 on an Italian railway enthusiasts' forum: Trenitalia sued the start-up behind a third-party app that showed train delays, downloaded on over 3 million phones; the app was suspended (forum, page 1).
- Opening question (IT): 'perché i dati di un servizio pubblico non possono essere resi open?' (EN: why can the data of a public service not be made open?) (post 1).
- One user (IT): 'ho l'imbarazzo della scelta per sapere dove si trova un aereo e devo consultare la sfera di cristallo per sapere dove si trova un treno' (EN: I am spoilt for choice to know where a plane is and I must look into a crystal ball to know where a train is) (post 5).
- Users explain the operator's reasons as reported by the press: a commercial service built without agreement, heavy load on the operator's systems, and showing only part of the offer. Several think real-time data should be published by the infrastructure manager, not by each operator, and note it does not do so (posts 4-5).
- Other apps showing delays exist and were not sued; users point to paid in-app features as the possible difference (posts 6-8).
- One user recalls using a third-party site that read delays to know 'quando andare in stazione' (EN: when to go to the station) because the official tools did not work on his phone (post 11).
- Second reading, pages 2 and 3 (27 posts, 5 August to 10 September 2019). Italian, working translation. A post reports Trenitalia's reply to the press: the app took data 'interrogando il sistema di vendita di Trenitalia' (by querying Trenitalia's sales system), 'ogni giorno circa 800mila richieste, con picchi che hanno superato le 14mila richieste ogni 10 minuti' (about 800,000 requests a day, with peaks above 14,000 every 10 minutes), and 'presentando un incompleto pacchetto di offerte di Trenitalia, l'app non offriva al cliente un confronto equilibrato' (by showing an incomplete set of Trenitalia offers, the app did not give the customer a balanced comparison) (post 16, 5 August 2019). This is a forum post quoting a company statement, not the statement itself
- Users on why they prefer the third-party app: 'l'app di trenitalia in confronto è fatta coi piedi. trenìt molto più fruibile ed intuitiva' (Trenitalia's app is badly made by comparison; Trenìt is much more usable and intuitive) (post 34); another agrees and names a second third-party timetable app (post 35). One user doubts the load argument: most requests through the app 'sia stata alternativa e non aggiuntiva' (replaced, and did not add to) those the same people would have sent through the official channels (post 22)
- Who should publish the data: 'La soluzione comunque potrebbe (dovrebbe?) essere un'interfaccia pubblica direttamente offerta da RFI' (the solution could, or should, be a public interface offered directly by the infrastructure manager), as in air travel where delay data come from airports and flight trackers, not from the airline (post 33). Another asks who pays for the infrastructure if third parties query it without agreement (post 18)
- Outcome reported in the thread: a post copies the app's public announcement that the Court of Rome rejected the request to suspend the service in the interim proceedings, that the app is back in the stores, was 'scaricata da oltre 3 milioni di utenti' (downloaded by over 3 million users) and lets travellers compare 'i diversi operatori ferroviari' (the different rail operators) (post 36, early September 2019); a reply warns that this is only the interim stage (post 38). The figure is the app's own claim
- Side remarks: delay compensation is paid late in one user's experience, 'sforano sempre, ma restano entro i 3 mesi' (they always overrun, but stay within three months) against the 30 days stated (post 26); the competitor's own train-status service is judged 'mediocre' while the incumbent's is 'eccellente dal punto di vista dei contenuti' (excellent in content) (posts 28-30)

## Limits

Enthusiast forum: the users are rail fans, many with technical knowledge, not typical commuters. 2019. All three pages were read. The outcome of the court case after September 2019 is not in the thread. Legal claims in the posts are opinions of non-lawyers. Shows that access to real-time train data in Italy was contested and that people use delay data to decide when to leave home.
