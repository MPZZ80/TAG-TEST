## What it says

Travel diaries written by German railway enthusiasts. They show from the inside what a chain of regional and long-distance trains is like: a connection of a few minutes decides the whole trip, a delay early in the day grows into hours when the last train of the evening is missed, and at the station nobody, staff included, knows what will happen. The same writers also enjoy the travel time and use it to study or work.

## Data and quotes used

- The forum describes the thread as the place for readers' reports and travel accounts ("Eure Reportagen und Reiseberichte finden hier ihren Platz"; in English: your reports and travel accounts find their place here; working translation).
- German railway forum, section for travel reports; page 4 of a long thread, written in 2016 by a handful of frequent rail travellers (thread page, captured 2026-10-07).
- A missed last connection turns a small delay into a big one: a traveller arrives in Munich 100 minutes late after a problem in Austria and reaches home about 120 minutes late because 'die letzte RB ab Stuttgart Hbf (00:15) nach Heilbronn halt keinen sinnvollen FV-Anschluss von Muenchen' has (EN: the last regional train from Stuttgart has no usable long-distance connection from Munich) (post of 22 May 2016).
- A 3-minute connection decides an 8-hour trip: the writer waits to buy the ticket hoping the official 3-minute connection is added to the planner; it is not, so the planned trip Munich-Dresden takes almost eight hours. A signal-box fault gives +24 minutes and 'dass ich meinen ausserplanmaessigen Anschluss vergessen kann' (EN: I can forget my unofficial connection). Final arrival +175 minutes, after 1 am (post of 15 June 2016).
- The planner is optimistic during a delay: 'Waehrend die Fahrplanauskunft optimistisch von einer Verspaetungsreduktion auf +17 bis Hof ausgeht, ist das Gegenteil der Fall' (EN: while the journey planner optimistically expects the delay to shrink to +17, the opposite happens): +30, +33, +40 (post of 15 June 2016).
- No information at the station: the train disappears from the platform display, 'hinterlaesst ratlose Fahrgaeste' (EN: leaves passengers at a loss); the driver and conductor 'sind genauso ratlos wie die ratsuchenden Fahrgaeste' (EN: are as lost as the passengers); the driver phones the control room and understands 15 minutes when 50 were said; the display later shows only a blue screen (post of 15 June 2016).
- Late information removes the alternative: after the announcement of a one-hour wait an older man shouts 'WAS SAGT DER DAS NICHT FRUEHER ... DANN HAETTE ICH JA UEBER GERA FAHREN KOENNEN' (EN: why did he not say so earlier, I could have gone via Gera), while the train to Gera is just leaving. Another passenger: 'Sagen Sie doch gleich, dass nichts mehr kommt. Dann wissen wir wenigstens Bescheid' (EN: tell us straight away that nothing is coming, then at least we know) (post of 15 June 2016).
- Effect on choice: a student who normally uses ride sharing took the train for a tax reason; on the way out a big delay forced him to take a taxi to arrive on time, at his own cost: 'Eigentlich habe er nach den beiden Fahrten ueberhaupt keine Lust mehr, mit der Bahn zu fahren' (EN: after the two trips he has no wish at all to take the train again). The writer agrees: last summer he never arrived with less than +30 (post of 15 June 2016).
- Passenger rights do not cover it: the regional day ticket gives nothing back, and the taxi is not refunded (post of 15 June 2016).
- Planner results people do not believe: a passenger is surprised that a trip with three changes is faster than the direct one; his partner notes 'das sind nur ganz kurze Umsteigezeiten ... nur 5 Minuten' (EN: those are very short changes, only 5 minutes) (post of 5 August 2016).
- Works on the line make a planned connection miss by three minutes: 50 minutes of waiting at the junction; the trip of 640 km takes over nine hours, chosen for the price (post of 5 August 2016).
- Last urban leg, Munich: with 11 minutes of planned change the writer tries a 1-minute one and misses the tram by 20 seconds; a stop display shows the next bus in 23 minutes while the one in the shelter shows 1 minute, and he bets on a display error (post of 7 August 2016). 'natuerlich faehrt die naechste Tram in 15 Minuten' at the end of a 6-hour trip (post of 4 July 2016).
- Travel time is used and valued: writers study, work on a paper or look out; one calls 6 hours 'angenehme' (EN: pleasant) but is 'nach 7 Uhr nicht mehr aufnahmefaehig' (EN: unable to take anything in after 7 pm) (posts of 4 July and 5 August 2016). The signature of one writer keeps yearly statistics of his own trips: 30,600 km, connections reached 89.1%, punctuality within 6 minutes in Germany 57.8%.
- Small frictions with tickets: no working ticket machine at a station, a queue too long to buy before departure; staff let people board and pay later (posts of 14 June 2016).

## Limits

Rail enthusiasts on leisure or weekend trips, Germany, 2016: not daily commuters, and far more tolerant and expert than an average traveller (they enjoy the trip and know the rules). Long literary posts by three or four people; one page of ten. Useful for the mechanics of connections and for the experience of a disruption at a station, which is the same for a commuter; not for how often it happens. Translations are working translations; quotes were shortened and dialect spelling normalised in two places, so check the original before reuse. Nicknames were not copied.
