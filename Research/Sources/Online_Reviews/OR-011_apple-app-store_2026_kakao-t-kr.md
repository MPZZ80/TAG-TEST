## What it says

A sample of 100 Korean App Store reviews of Kakao T, about 70% one-star. Although the app sells taxi, bike, parking, intercity bus, train and flights, the reviews are almost all about taxi hailing: no taxi found unless the paid tier is chosen, taxis assigned from 8-20 minutes away, penalties for cancelling, and routes that reviewers find longer than those of other map apps. Nobody in the sample writes about combining modes, or about the bus and train sections. Users say they keep using it because there is no alternative.

## Data and quotes used

- Store rating on 2026-10-07: 4.71 average from 464,055 ratings in the South Korea store; app version 7.14.0, released 2026-09-16 (Apple lookup).
- Sample: 100 reviews, 5 stars: 15, 4 stars: 3, 3 stars: 6, 2 stars: 7, 1 stars: 69.
- Sample: 100 reviews, App Store South Korea, 2026-09-05 to 2026-10-04; ratings: 69 one-star, 7 two-star, 6 three-star, 3 four-star, 15 five-star, and several five-star reviews are sarcastic complaints (store sample). My count by theme, approximate: about 45 on taxi dispatch (none found, far away, cancelled by the driver); about 18 on the route the navigation gives; about 12 on being pushed to a paid taxi tier; about 8 on shared bikes and scooters (return zones, position errors, charges); about 4 on monthly parking; none on intercity bus, train or flights.
- Why people take a taxi, repeated in many reviews: they are late. KO: '택시는 급할 때 이용하는 교통수단 아닌가요?' (EN: is a taxi not a means of transport one uses when in a hurry?) (2026-09-13); '바쁘니까 택시를 잡죠. 5분 10분이 급하니까 ... 10분 거리 기사를 배정하면 뭐 어쩌라는겁니까?' (EN: we take a taxi because we are in a hurry, 5 or 10 minutes matter ... what are we supposed to do if a driver 10 minutes away is assigned?) (2026-09-22).
- The fallback fails and people go back to the first plan: after repeated failed dispatches 'in the end I took the bus' (2026-10-02); 'I was going to be late so I got off the subway and called a taxi, and I was later than the subway' (2026-09-05); 'if I had known I would have taken the bus' (2026-09-13). Mornings are the worst: 'in the morning I tried for 30 minutes and was late' (2026-09-06).
- Waiting time shown is not kept: 'it says 3 minutes and I regularly wait more than double' (2026-09-18); 'a taxi that should arrive in 5 minutes has not come after 10' (2026-10-02).
- Asking before deciding for the user: a reviewer was assigned cars 7-8 and then 20 minutes away five times in a row and then banned for 24 hours for cancelling; he proposes the app should ask first 'the fastest car is 20 minutes away, do you still want it?' and warn clearly before a penalty (2026-09-05).
- Route chosen by the app: several say the app's navigation sends the taxi on a longer or more congested route than other map apps ('40 minutes there, over an hour here') and that the passenger pays for it; one notes that the same company's map app shows a faster route that the taxi app does not offer (2026-09-15, 09-05, 09-13). One asks to see afterwards which route was taken (2026-09-22).
- Pick-up point: the app moves the point the user set, or the driver is sent to another exit or a side street (2026-10-04, 09-28, 09-21, 09-12).
- Lock-in is stated: drivers and passengers use it 'because there is no alternative'; several say they wait for a foreign competitor to be allowed, or name a public transport card's taxi service as the alternative (2026-09-19, 09-18, 09-26, 09-23).
- Shared bikes and scooters: return zones few and far, position errors that prevent the return so the charge keeps running ('20,000 won taken'), bikes not where the map shows them; one uses the bike 'only for going home from work' (2026-09-29, 09-22, 09-19, 09-16, 09-08).
- Monthly parking sold in the app: the barrier did not recognise the pass for ten days, making the reviewer 10-20 minutes late leaving work each day; help only through chat (2026-09-26).
- Advertising in an urgent moment: 'I am in a hurry and I have to close two or three ad windows' (2026-09-13).

## Limits

South Korea; a taxi market dominated by one app under dispute with drivers and regulators, with no equivalent in Italy. The sample says nothing about the multi-modal side of the app, so it cannot show whether one app for all modes works for commuters. App store reviews over-represent anger; star ratings are unreliable here because several five-star reviews are complaints. Counts by theme are my rough reading of 100 items. Korean slang read through my working translation: the quotes should be checked by a Korean reader before reuse; where I was unsure I paraphrased in English only.
