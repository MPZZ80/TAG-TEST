# Survey Results: First Export

What the commuter survey says so far, from the exports of 19 October 2026. The counts are of the answers received; the last sections, marked as such, are the agent's reading.

## In short

- **21 answers arrived; 18 are kept.** Three are excluded as probable automated answers (see "Excluded answers").
- **17 of the 18 kept answers come from Belgium**, 16 in French and 1 in Dutch, sent after the Belgian associations shared the survey. One comes from "another European country". No valid answer comes from Italy, Germany, the Netherlands, Spain or the United Kingdom.
- **So the differences between countries cannot be measured yet.** What can be said is what the Belgian respondents report, and where they differ from the three Italian interviews (section "Belgian answers against the interviews").
- **The Belgian picture:** long trips (8 of 17 between 90 and 120 minutes), mostly regional train plus walking, two or three office days a week, trips disrupted once or twice a month or more. The service itself is what bothers them most (11 of 17), then late or wrong information (7) and no alternative when a train is cancelled (6).
- **Checking does not help them find out in time.** 9 of 17 always check before leaving, yet only 1 of the 17 learned of the last problem before leaving home; 7 learned it at the station and 4 say nobody told them.

### Open answers

Quotes are verbatim, in French, with a working English translation.

**What they would change (question 23), grouped by the agent**

- **Fewer changes, a direct train (4):** "Un train direct sans correspondance" (a direct train with no change); "Pas de correspondance" (no change); "Avoir un train direct et donc un trajet raccourci" (a direct train and so a shorter trip); "Les correspondances" (the connections).
- **The first leg from the village (2):** "Plus de bus dans les villages pour rejoindre la gare" (more buses in the villages to reach the station); "Plus de fréquence de bus entre mon village et ma première gare" (more frequent buses between my village and my first station), the same person adding the end of cancelled peak trains in school holidays.
- **The service (4):** "La ponctualité des trains" (punctual trains); "Revenir au service SNCB antérieur au covid" (go back to the railway's service before covid); "Ajouter des wagons à mon IC le matin" (add coaches to my morning intercity); "Être certaine du maintien de mon arrêt de train" (to be sure my train stop is kept).
- **Other (3):** smoother trips; priority to national over international trains; "Supprimer les voitures" (remove cars).

**Their own tricks (question 24)**

- **Leaving early:** "Avoir 5 minutes d'avance de sécurité sur l'horaire" (keep 5 minutes of safety margin); "Partir tôt pour ne pas arriver en retard" (leave early so as not to arrive late).
- **Accepting it and using the time:** "rater les correspondances ne m'ennuie pas plus que ça. Au pire, j'ai gagné une demie heure de lecture" (missing connections does not bother me much; at worst I gained half an hour of reading); "J'ai toujours un livre et qqchose à grignoter dans mon sac, au cas où" (I always have a book and something to eat in my bag, just in case); "Zen. Le principal est d'arriver" (calm; what matters is to arrive); "Avoir de la compagnie ou lâcher prise" (have company or let go).
- **Feeling safe on the way:** two women describe the walk between station and office or the wait at the station as the moment they protect themselves, by walking with others or keeping away from people they find threatening.

**About the survey itself (1):** a cyclist with no problems found the questions on disruptions compulsory and useless for him, and missed a motorcycle option.

### The one answer from outside Belgium

"Another European country", once a week or more, 30–59 minutes, car only, no change, four office days fixed by the employer. Checks a traffic app before leaving; the last problem was traffic, met during the trip, and he waited. Did not know automatic alerts were possible. Bothered by traffic, parking and cost.

## Belgian answers against the interviews (agent's reading)

The three interviews are Italian commuters; the survey is Belgian. The methods differ, so these are contrasts to check, not measured differences between countries.

| Topic | Italian interviews (3) | Belgian survey (17) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Checking before leaving | None of the three checks on a normal day | 9 always check |
| Finding out about a problem | At the station | Mostly at the station too (7), or nobody told them (4), even among those who check |
| Consequence of arriving late | None for any of the three | A real problem for 4 of 17 |
| Fallback | Each has one: the car, another train, staying home | 8 of 17 have none; 13 simply waited last time |
| What they ask for | To be told earlier about their own line | Mostly a better service: punctuality, direct trains, buses to the station |
| Alerts | One participant's friend relies on them | 8 do not want them, 5 did not know they exist, 1 finds them useful |

## What it adds to the open issues (agent's reading)

The issues are those of [open_issues.md](../open_issues.md).

- **Q-01, fixed hours:** answered in part. 4 Belgian respondents say a 20-minute delay is a real problem; the interviews had none.
- **Q-02, combining services:** 6 Belgian respondents change once and 6 change two or more times; the change is the part that costs most effort for 6.
- **Q-04, early warning:** checking before leaving does not mean knowing in time: of 9 who always check, 1 knew before leaving. Few use alerts, and most of the others do not want them.
- **Q-05, travel days:** two or three days a week is the most common; the employer fixes them for 6 of 17.
- **Q-08, which source they believe:** split between the operator's app (5), station screens (4) and staff (3).
- **W-01, few and similar participants:** the survey adds 17 people outside Marco's circle, but almost all from one country and one language community, recruited through one users' association.

## Limits

- 18 answers, 17 from one country. Counts describe these respondents, not Belgian commuters.
- Respondents were reached through a Belgian rail users' association and its social media: people who use the train and care about it are over-represented.
- Of the 17, 2 have trips under 30 minutes, below the intercity range of the research; they are kept in the counts.
- Questions 17 to 19 were compulsory also for those with no problem, as one respondent pointed out, so some answers there may be forced.
- The country is the one the respondent chose; the language of the form does not tell the region.
