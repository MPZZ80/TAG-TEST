# Deep-Dive Interview Script (Draft)

Draft list of topics for interviews with fellow intercity commuters. The interviews follow the commute door to door: from the moment the participant leaves home to the moment they arrive at their destination, and back. Question numbers (Q1–Q40) refer to `../Questions/initial_questions.md`. Probes marked **[after desk research]** will be added once the findings in `08_synthesis.md` are available.

## Format

- **Length:** 30–45 minutes.
- **Participants:** 5–6 commuters, mixed by travel frequency, main mode, route familiarity and time pressure.
- **Style:** semi-structured. The topics below are a checklist, not a questionnaire. Follow the participant's story and return to the list for what is missing.
- **Core technique:** ask about specific recent trips ("tell me about the last time…"), not about general opinions or what they would like in an app.

## Before starting

- Explain the purpose: understanding how people commute between cities, from door to door, not testing a product.
- Say there are no right or wrong answers.
- Ask for consent to record and explain how notes will be used.
- Do not mention any solution, feature or technology.

## Topics

### 1. Commuter profile and context (Q1–Q8) — about 5 min

- Where they travel from and to, door to door, and why. The kind of place at each end (city centre, suburb, small town) is enough; no addresses.
- How often, on which days, and whether that varies.
- How long they have been doing this commute.
- Fixed constraints: arrival times, family duties, cost, accessibility, things to carry.
- Mobility options available for the first mile, the main leg and the last mile, and options they exclude.
- Other people who affect their travel choices.

### 2. A typical commute, door to door (Q9–Q16) — about 8 min

- Walk through the most recent normal commute, from closing the door at home to arriving at the destination, step by step. Then the return trip, if it differs.
- Each leg and the mode used: first mile, main intercity leg, transfers, last mile.
- How long each leg takes, how long the whole journey takes, and how much that varies.
- Connections between legs: parking, waiting, tickets, walking.
- The leg that causes the most stress or uncertainty, and the one that works best.
- What is routine and no longer needs thinking.
- What they like and dislike about this routine.

### 3. Planning and deciding how to travel (Q9–Q16) — about 7 min

- When and how they decide how to travel on a given day, and whether they decide for the whole journey at once or leg by leg.
- How the first and last mile influence the choice of the main mode.
- What they check before leaving, where, and in which order.
- How they choose between public transport, car and other services.
- What matters most: time, cost, reliability, comfort, flexibility, habit.
- The last time they changed their usual mode, and what triggered it.
- How planning differs for an unusual trip (new place, different time, strike, bad weather).

### 4. Information during the journey (Q17–Q19) — about 5 min

- What they need to know on each leg and at which moments, including the first and last mile.
- Where they get it: apps, displays, announcements, other people.
- Sources they trust and sources they stopped trusting, and why.
- Times when information was missing, late, unclear or contradictory.

### 5. When something goes wrong (Q20–Q23) — about 8 min

- The last time a commute did not go as planned: what happened, on which leg, step by step.
- How the problem affected the rest of the journey, such as a missed connection or no way to cover the last mile.
- How and when they found out.
- What they did next, which alternatives they considered, how they chose.
- What helped and what was missing at that moment.
- Consequences for them and for others, and how it felt.
- How often this kind of thing happens.
- The worst commute they remember.

### 6. Tools, services and workarounds (Q24–Q26) — about 5 min

- Apps, websites and services they use, what each one is for, and which leg of the journey it covers.
- Legs for which they have no tool at all.
- Ask them to show their phone and the apps involved, if comfortable.
- How they move from one tool to another during a trip, and what is annoying about it.
- Informal channels: group chats, colleagues, station staff, social media.
- Personal rules and habits that protect them from problems (leaving earlier, backup routes, a preferred carriage or car park).
- Tools they tried and abandoned, and why.
- **[after desk research]** Reactions to the gaps found in existing aggregator apps.

### 7. Why existing solutions fall short (Q27–Q31) — about 3 min

- What they expect from current tools that they do not get.
- Whether they have ever looked for something better, and what they found.
- What a tool would need to know about them and their commute to be useful.

### 8. What a good commute looks like (Q34–Q39) — about 4 min

- A commute that went particularly well from door to door, and what made it so.
- What they would change first about their commute if they could.
- Decisions they want to keep for themselves, and decisions they would gladly hand over.
- How they would notice their commute had improved.

### 9. Closing — about 2 min

- Anything important that was not asked.
- Who else they would suggest talking to.
- Permission for a short follow-up.

## After each interview

- Write down the three most surprising points within the hour.
- Note direct quotes with the topic number.
- Mark which of Q1–Q40 received new evidence.
- Note what to ask differently in the next interview.
