# Desk Research: Partial Analysis

What the collected sources say about the 40 research questions, after reading all the material that could be read. It gives the answer the evidence supports for each question, says how solid it is, and lists what stays open for the interviews.

It is still called partial for one reason: the activity outputs (`01_…` to `07_…`) and the synthesis in `plan.md` have not been written. The reading is no longer partial.

- **What was read:** every text copy in `docs_md/`, from start to end or by chapter. Of the 132 sources, 107 were read: 90 in full and 17 by chapter or in part. What "by chapter" covers is written in the "Read" row of each source file and in the ledger in `progress.md`.
- **What could not be read:** 25 sources. For 20 papers the publisher and the open repository refused the download, so only the abstract in the catalogue was read, and for some there is no abstract. Five web pages are built by scripts and could not be captured. They are listed under "Limits".
- **Where the evidence is:** in the "Data and quotes used" section of each source file, with page numbers. This file only points to it.
- **How to read a finding:** each has a number (F1, F2…), its sources, the leg of the journey it concerns and a confidence level derived from the grades of the sources. Numbers F1 to F32 are the same as in the earlier version of this file; F33 to F52 are new.
- **Translations** from German, French, Dutch, Spanish, Japanese and Korean are working translations by the agent. Figures read from chart images are marked in the source files.

## Summary

1. **Commuters use one means of transport, and mostly the car.** In Italy 84.6% of commuters always use a single means, and only 4.8% combine public and private means (F33). The same pattern holds in Madrid, Switzerland and the EU (F7, F34). Combining is more common on longer trips and on trips that leave the municipality, which is where this project looks (F35).
2. **The intercity commuter is a small group.** In Italy 4% of workers and students travel more than 50 km to work or study, and 2.2% of all trips exceed 50 km (F36). In Germany 1 to 2% of adults commute over 100 km (F37). No official statistic describes this group door to door.
3. **Working from home goes with longer commutes.** In Germany workers who can work from home live 22 km from work against 15 km, and the distance grows with the days at home (F38). In Japan half of those who commute 90 minutes or more telework, against 13.5% of those under 30 minutes (F39). Presence at the workplace concentrates in the middle of the week (F40). Season tickets built on five days a week fit badly (F41).
4. **In a disruption, what people lack is what to do next and an honest reason.** Only 37% of delayed British rail passengers were satisfied with the information on alternatives and connections (F20). App reviews in four countries describe the same thing from inside: alternatives that cannot be travelled, delay messages that are late or wrong in both directions, and the status of the current trip lost when looking for another (F42, F43).
5. **People decide how to travel before they open an app.** In the Sydney trial 99% of trips were made without the integrated app, and participants had already chosen the mode before checking a timetable (F44). Regular travellers use an app to check their usual trip at a glance: saved trips, platform, delay (F45).
6. **The service matters more than the tool.** Italians name punctuality as the first thing that would make them use public transport more (F46). Users blame apps for the failures of the service behind them (F47). This was the hypothesis of F31 and it is now better supported.
7. **A change between two services costs far more than its minutes**, and planners propose the tightest change, which regular travellers do not trust (F16, F48).
8. **"Everything in one app" has not found its users.** Demand is unproven at scale, most pilots ended, and the Italian national programme reached about 59,000 users with 56.9 million euros (F27 to F30, F49).
9. **Integration is blocked by rules and interests more than by technology.** The European rules proposed in 2026 leave urban transport out, so the first and last legs stay outside the single ticket; real-time data were optional until 2024; incumbents hold 80 to 90% of online rail sales (F25, F26, F50).
10. **The evidence is still weakest where the brief is most specific:** the door-to-door journey of someone who commutes between cities, leg by leg, and what they decide each day. Seven questions have no material at all.

## Evidence against the brief's assumptions

The brief assumes that commuters combine modes and that fragmented information is the core problem. The complete reading makes the first assumption weaker and changes the second.

- **Few commuters combine modes** (F6, F33, F34), and people who do often use a means they dislike (F51).
- **Routine trips need little information.** Frequent travellers want to know about deviations from the normal, not how to get there (F45). For regular trips "users have established habits, routes and destinations" (F44).
- **An integrated planner is hardly used even when it is free and subsidised** (F44, F49).
- **Most people who book combined journeys say it is easy**, and the Commission's own estimate of what better ticketing could add to rail is 1.7% (F14, F15).
- **Reliability of the service comes before information** (F46, F47, F31).
- **Habit.** 22% of Europeans say they find it hard to change habits (F13); people have a main means and a predetermined fallback (F44); temporary incentives do not last (F52).
- **What does support the brief:** in a disruption the information on what to do next is missing or wrong (F20, F42, F43), tickets do not carry over between services (F50), and travellers describe being tired of moving between apps (F29).

## Answers by question

Status: **partial** means verified evidence exists; **weak** means indirect evidence only; **open** means nothing was found.

### Who (Q1–Q8)

**Q1. Who commutes between cities, why, and what does the journey look like door to door?** Partial.

- **F1.** In the EU-27 in 2019, 61.3% of workers reached work in less than 30 minutes, 26.3% in 30 to 59 minutes and 8.1% in 60 minutes or more. The average was 25 minutes in the EU and 21 in Italy, among the shortest. Commuting time grows with education. Source: [GR-001](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-001_eurostat_2020_commuting-time.md). Leg: whole journey. Confidence: medium.
- **F2.** In Italy in 2019, 12 million employed people and 3.5 million students travelled outside their municipality every day; 900,000 people took a train daily and 1 million several times a week. In Lombardy more than two employed people in three work in another municipality. In the EU 12.5 million people commuted to another region in 2022, mostly around capitals (this last figure was read through an automatic page summary). Sources: [GR-023](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-023_istat_2020_spostamenti-sul-territorio-prima-del-covid.md) (PDF p.1, p.3), [GR-002](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-002_eurostat_2023_interregional-commuters.md). Leg: whole journey. Confidence: medium.
- **F36.** Distance from home to work or study in Italy: 4% travel more than 50 km, 37% between 10 and 50 km, 46% between 1 and 10 km. In Europe 7% travel more than 50 km. Trips over 50 km are 2.2% of all trips in Italy (2.6% in 2019); the average trip outside the municipality is 25.1 km and 32 minutes. Sources: [GR-017](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-017_ipsos-europ-assistance_2025_mobility-barometer.md) (PDF p.12, read from the chart image), [GR-008](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-008_isfort_2025_rapporto-mobilita-22-sintesi.md) (PDF p.4–6). Leg: whole journey. Confidence: medium (one commercial survey and one research institute, different measures that agree).
- **F37.** In Germany, with a threshold of 100 km, 1 to 2% of adults are long-distance commuters, some only occasionally; 45% of them cover 100 to 250 km; most go by car and long-distance rail also plays a role. The survey calls the trip "keine wirkliche Privatreise, keine echte Geschäftsreise" (not really a private trip, not a true business trip) and says it is hard to see in a one-day diary. 23% of long-distance train trips are commutes. Source: [GR-041](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-041_bmv-infas_2025_mobilitaet-in-deutschland-2023.md) (long report p.19, p.111, p.229). Confidence: medium.
- Still open: the door-to-door journey itself. Statistics assign each trip one main means and one distance band (F34), so the legs are invisible.

**Q2. How do commuters differ by frequency?** Partial, much better covered than before.

- **F3.** Working from home every day fell from 24.6% of the employed in Italy in 2020 to 4% in 2024, while remote workers stabilised at just over 3.5 million (570,000 in 2019): hybrid work replaced full remote work. 64% of Italian workers never work from home; those who do average 0.9 days a week. Shares with some home working: 42% of German workers have the option, 43% of Dutch workers do at least an hour a week, 24.6% of Japanese employees telework. Sources: [GR-008](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-008_isfort_2025_rapporto-mobilita-22-sintesi.md) (PDF p.15), [GR-017](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-017_ipsos-europ-assistance_2025_mobility-barometer.md) (PDF p.13), [GR-041](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-041_bmv-infas_2025_mobilitaet-in-deutschland-2023.md) (p.211), [GR-043](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-043_kim_2024_kerncijfers-mobiliteit-2024.md) (PDF p.19), [GR-048](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-048_mlit_2025_telework-jinkou-jittai-chousa-r6.md) (PDF p.14). Confidence: high.
- **F4.** Commuting has not returned to its level before the pandemic: commute trips in Germany fell 13% against 2017 although employment grew; commuting distance in the Netherlands is 16% below 2019; in the US about 9 million fewer workers leave home in the morning core hours; Italian regional rail lost 11% of passengers from 2019 to 2024, which the regulator links to commuters travelling fewer days a week. Sources: [GR-041](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-041_bmv-infas_2025_mobilitaet-in-deutschland-2023.md) (p.68), [GR-043](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-043_kim_2024_kerncijfers-mobiliteit-2024.md) (PDF p.19), [GR-031](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-031_us-census-bureau_2024_commuting-in-the-us-2022.md) (PDF p.7), [GR-008](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-008_isfort_2025_rapporto-mobilita-22-sintesi.md) (PDF p.4), [GR-003](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-003_art_2025_relazione-annuale.md). Confidence: high.
- **F38.** In Germany the distance to the workplace is 15 km for workers without home office and 22 km with it, and it grows with the days at home: 18 km for less than one day a week, 20 for one to two, 24 for two to three, 26 for three or more. The survey reads this as home office being used "to avoid especially long commutes" and adds that longer distances are probably accepted more easily when there are fewer trips. In Switzerland workers who can work at home make fewer but longer work trips. Sources: [GR-041](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-041_bmv-infas_2025_mobilitaet-in-deutschland-2023.md) (table 55, p.213–214), [GR-035](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-035_bfs-are_2023_mikrozensus-mobilitaet-verkehr-2021.md) (PDF p.48). Leg: whole journey. Confidence: high (two official surveys agree).
- **F39.** In Japan the share of employees who telework rises with the one-way commute: 13.5% under 30 minutes, 29.2% for 30 to 60, 43.3% for 60 to 90, 50.6% for 90 minutes or more. It is 42.7% among rail and bus commuters and 13.2% among car commuters. In the Madrid region 41.5% of teleworkers in the outer ring work from home five days a week, against 20.3% in central Madrid; the report reads it as "a stable solution to avoid long trips". Sources: [GR-048](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-048_mlit_2025_telework-jinkou-jittai-chousa-r6.md) (PDF p.14–15, chart images), [GR-045](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-045_crtm_2025_encuesta-sintetica-movilidad-madrid-2024.md). Confidence: high. None of these surveys can say which comes first, the long commute or the home working.
- **F40.** Presence concentrates in the middle of the week. In Madrid telework is highest on Monday and Friday (about 21%) and lowest on Thursday (16.9%). In Germany almost 10 million fewer commute trips are made on an average Friday than in 2017, and the smallest fall is on Wednesday. Teleworkers in Japan average 2.1 days a week, and most would like more. Sources: [GR-045](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-045_crtm_2025_encuesta-sintetica-movilidad-madrid-2024.md) (PDF p.55), [GR-041](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-041_bmv-infas_2025_mobilitaet-in-deutschland-2023.md) (p.68–69), [GR-048](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-048_mlit_2025_telework-jinkou-jittai-chousa-r6.md) (PDF p.19, p.23). Confidence: medium.
- **F41.** Passes built on five days a week fit hybrid commuters badly. Great Britain introduced a ticket for any 8 days in 28 in 2021, because "the idea of travelling 5 days a week to the office is fast becoming a relic of the past"; a commentator called it "a bulk purchase of tickets", not a season ticket. In the Sydney trial 56% stayed on pay-as-you-go because they travel too little, cannot estimate next month's travel and dislike committing in advance. ISFORT warns that remote work can reduce the demand for season tickets. Sources: [GR-052](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-052_govuk_2021_flexible-season-tickets-announcement.md), [AR-009](Sources/Articles/AR-009_personnel-today_2021_flexible-rail-tickets-hybrid.md), [GR-037](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-037_imove-australia_2021_sydney-maas-trial-final-report.md) (PDF p.72–73), [GR-008](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-008_isfort_2025_rapporto-mobilita-22-sintesi.md) (PDF p.43). Confidence: medium.
- Still open: which days Italian intercity commuters travel, who decides them, and what they buy.

**Q3. How does familiarity with a route change planning?** Partial.

- **F5.** In a route-choice experiment only 10% of people chose the fastest route every time. Source: [AP-022](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-022_ramirez_2020_travel-time-bounded-rationality.md) (abstract only). Confidence: low.
- **F45.** Frequent travellers "may have little need for navigation information, but a greater interest in learning about variations from the expected performance". Regular users of railway apps say the same in their own words: saved trips are "the only reason for using the app instead of google maps"; "I'm not a tourist"; in the morning they want the platform "without an extra action". When updates removed saved trips or hid the platform, a quarter of the Dutch sample and a fifth of the Swiss one complained, and both operators reversed the change. A British commuter asks for a standing alert on the two trains he takes every day. Sources: [GR-040](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-040_trb_2003_tcrp-92-traveler-information.md) (PDF p.28), [OR-008](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-008_apple-app-store_2026_ns-app-nl.md), [OR-014](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-014_apple-app-store_2026_sbb-mobile-ch.md), [OR-005](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-005_apple-app-store_2026_trainline-gb.md) (2026-09-18). Leg: main leg, transfer. Confidence: medium (a research review and many independent reviews in three countries; the review is from 2003).

**Q4. What constraints shape the journey?** Weak.

- Chains of activities and people in care push toward the car: people with many daily activities or little flexibility on arrival time are less likely to use public transport, and having a person in care lowers bus commuting ([AP-001](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-001_goransson_2023_factors-that-make-public.md), PDF p.9). Women's trips are more complex and cost more when several tickets are needed ([GR-009](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-009_oecd-itf_2021_integrating-public-transport-into-maas.md), PDF p.13). Transport takes 10.8% of the Italian household budget ([GR-004](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-004_legambiente_2025_pendolaria-20-edizione.md), PDF p.50). Confidence: low. A paper on acceptable travel time ([AP-020](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-020_milakis_2018_me-it-always-like.md)) could not be obtained.

**Q5. Which options does each commuter have on each leg?** Open. Evidence on supply exists (half of Germans have poor public transport at home, F46), not on what one commuter can choose from on each leg.

**Q6. Who combines several modes and who uses one?** Partial, well covered.

- **F6.** In Italy trips made with a combination of motorised means were 2.8% of motorised trips in the first half of 2025, 3.2% in 2024 and 6.5% in 2019; higher in the North-West and in large cities. ISFORT concludes that such behaviour "remains entirely marginal: the average user tends to prefer a single mode, often out of habit or lack of information". Source: [GR-008](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-008_isfort_2025_rapporto-mobilita-22-sintesi.md) (PDF p.8–9, p.48). Confidence: medium. The measure may not count a walk or a bike ride to the station.
- **F33.** In Italy in 2017, 84.6% of commuters always used one single means. More than one means: 11.2% of the employed and 34.4% of students. Public and private combined: 4.8% of commuters, 4.1% of the employed; highest in the outskirts of metropolitan areas (6.3%). Among the employed 73.7% use only private means. Source: [GR-024](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-024_istat_2018_spostamenti-quotidiani-e-nuove-forme-di-mobilita.md) (PDF p.1, p.5, p.7). Leg: whole journey. Confidence: medium (official statistics, 2017).
- **F7.** In the Madrid region 91.3% of trips have one leg; combining a private vehicle with another means is 0.1% of trips. In Switzerland 82% of trips have one leg, walking plus public transport is a good 9%, and car or bike plus public transport are each under 1%. A 2015 EU study found 78% use one mode for their most frequent trip. Sources: [GR-045](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-045_crtm_2025_encuesta-sintetica-movilidad-madrid-2024.md) (PDF p.42–43), [GR-035](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-035_bfs-are_2023_mikrozensus-mobilitaet-verkehr-2021.md) (PDF p.26, p.31), [GR-009](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-009_oecd-itf_2021_integrating-public-transport-into-maas.md) (PDF p.14). Confidence: high.
- **F35.** Combining rises with distance. In Italy most trips made with more than one means are by people who leave their municipality (68.7%, my reading of the sentence). In Madrid public transport takes 70.9% of trips of 61 to 90 minutes against 2.4% under 10 minutes. In Switzerland rail is the main means for 23% of outings over 50 km. In England 58% of public transport trips use more than one mode and 64% of those start on foot. In the Netherlands the multimodal group that uses the train makes twice as many trip segments as car users and is young, urban and often without a car. Sources: [GR-024](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-024_istat_2018_spostamenti-quotidiani-e-nuove-forme-di-mobilita.md) (PDF p.7), [GR-045](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-045_crtm_2025_encuesta-sintetica-movilidad-madrid-2024.md) (table 23), [GR-035](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-035_bfs-are_2023_mikrozensus-mobilitaet-verkehr-2021.md) (PDF p.29), [GR-030](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-030_dft_2025_national-travel-survey-2024.md), [AP-024](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-024_an_2022_multimodal-travel-behaviour-attitudes.md) (table 3). Confidence: medium.
- **F34.** Official statistics hide combined trips. Germany assigns a trip to one main means by a fixed order; France and the US record only the main means; Australia collects up to three methods and publishes one; Eurostat does not report trips between cities. Only the Swiss, Dutch and Madrid surveys count legs. Sources: [GR-041](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-041_bmv-infas_2025_mobilitaet-in-deutschland-2023.md) (short report p.34), [GR-042](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-042_sdes_2023_mobilite-locale-et-longue-distance-des-francais.md) (PDF p.8), [GR-031](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-031_us-census-bureau_2024_commuting-in-the-us-2022.md), [GR-034](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-034_abs_2022_australias-journey-to-work.md), [GR-016](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-016_eurostat_2021_passenger-mobility-statistics.md). Confidence: high. This is why the project's user is hard to count.
- **F51.** People who mix modes often use one they dislike. In the Netherlands the car is the best liked means in every group, including the multimodal train users (car 18.3, train 10.2 on the same scale); the odds of using a disliked means are 6 to 10 times higher for multimodal public transport users than for car users; the authors write that "multimodal individuals in general use modes they dislike" and may move toward the car. In Ghent only 17.9% of public transport users prefer it, and people who travel with their preferred means are more satisfied whatever it is. Sources: [AP-024](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-024_an_2022_multimodal-travel-behaviour-attitudes.md) (PDF p.7–10), [AP-018](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-018_vos_2018_people-travel-their-preferred.md) (PDF p.15–18). Confidence: medium (two studies; one is on leisure trips in one city).
- Still open: why the Italian share fell after 2019, and what holds a combination together for those who keep it.

**Q7. Who else is involved in the travel decisions?** Open. Indirect signs only: employers set the days (F40), and a Dutch reward app raised the worry that the employer could see the trips ([OR-016](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-016_apple-app-store_2026_reiswijs-nl.md)).

**Q8. Which groups experience the most friction?** Partial.

- **F8.** Commuters are the least satisfied rail passengers in Great Britain: 41% satisfied with how a delay was handled against 55% of leisure travellers; value for money 49% against 67%. Source: [GR-051](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-051_transport-focus_2026_rail-customer-experience-survey.md). Leg: main leg. Confidence: medium.
- **F9.** Barriers to multimodal travel differ by place: everywhere the synchronising of timetables and the reliability of each leg; in rural areas basic provision; in urban areas the complexity of planning. Source: [AP-015](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-015_mcilroy_2023_this-where-public-transport.md) (abstract only; the full text could not be obtained). Confidence: low.
- **F10.** On some Italian commuter lines the service itself fails often: on the Roma Nord line a commuters' committee counted more than 8,000 cancelled runs in ten months of 2025; on the former Circumvesuviana a student whose train should arrive at 7:51 arrives between 8:10 and 8:20 and takes an earlier train on exam days. Source: [GR-004](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-004_legambiente_2025_pendolaria-20-edizione.md) (PDF p.40). Confidence: low (advocacy report). The regulator's figures are far better (punctuality 89 to 97%) but count a train as late only after 5 minutes at destination and do not count a cancelled train replaced within half an hour ([GR-003](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-003_art_2025_relazione-annuale.md), PDF p.122, p.216).
- Other groups named: people in peripheral areas, who spend more on a car or more time on public transport ([GR-010](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-010_egum_2024_public-transport-shared-mobility.md), PDF p.8); people with reduced mobility, for whom only a door-to-door service is usable ([GR-046](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-046_digital-agency-japan_2025_idou-juyou-no-haaku-saishu-houkokusho.md), PDF p.26).

### Planning before leaving (Q9–Q12)

**Q9. How and when do people decide how to travel?** Weak, no longer open.

- **F44.** The means is decided before any tool is opened. In the Sydney trial "participants overwhelmingly already decide their mode of transport before using a journey planner to check the schedule"; 99% of trips were made without the trial app; many never opened its planner. British research found that people have a primary means and fall back on a predetermined default. For routine trips "users have established habits, routes and destinations". Sources: [GR-037](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-037_imove-australia_2021_sydney-maas-trial-final-report.md) (PDF p.49, p.77–78), [GR-040](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-040_trb_2003_tcrp-92-traveler-information.md) (PDF p.23), [GR-009](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-009_oecd-itf_2021_integrating-public-transport-into-maas.md) (PDF p.14), [AP-017](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-017_vos_2017_travel-satisfaction-revisited-pivotal.md) (PDF p.12). Leg: whole journey. Confidence: medium (urban commuters in Sydney, a 2003 review, an expert round table).
- Still open: when the decision is made on a given day, and whether for the whole journey or leg by leg.

**Q10. Routine trip compared with an unusual one?** Weak. For an unfamiliar trip people need an itinerary; for a familiar one, the deviations (F45). Planners are praised for unknown places: "Everyone is a local with this" ([OR-003](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-003_apple-app-store_2026_citymapper-gb.md)). Nothing describes how a commuter prepares for an unusual day.

**Q11. What information do people look for before leaving, and where?** Partial.

- **F11.** Before going to the station 38% of British rail passengers looked for nothing and "just turned up"; 43% checked live departures for punctuality or platform; 68% of those who looked used an app. What a commuter can still change before leaving is the means, the route and the departure time, and what they want is news of disruptions and delays. Vendors build for the repeated trip (saved searches, favourite routes, commuter sections). Sources: [GR-051](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-051_transport-focus_2026_rail-customer-experience-survey.md) (section 5), [GR-040](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-040_trb_2003_tcrp-92-traveler-information.md) (table 1), [PR-009](Sources/Products/PR-009_trenitalia_2026_trenitalia-app.md), [PR-007](Sources/Products/PR-007_db-fernverkehr_2026_db-navigator.md), [PR-014](Sources/Products/PR-014_sbb_2026_sbb-mobile.md). Confidence: medium.
- People use live data to decide when to leave home: a Dutch reviewer had learned to wait at home watching the bus position; an Italian forum user checked delays "to know when to go to the station"; a FlixBus customer left later after a delay notice and found the bus on time. Sources: [OR-015](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-015_apple-app-store_2026_9292-nl.md), [OF-001](Sources/Online_Forums/OF-001_ferrovie-it-forum_2019_ferrovie-it-trenitalia-sues-delay-app.md), [OR-006](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-006_apple-app-store_2026_flixbus-it.md). Confidence: low.

**Q12. Which legs are planned carefully and which are left to chance?** Open.

### Comparing and combining options (Q13–Q16)

**Q13. How do commuters choose between modes?** Partial.

- **F12.** The car dominates everywhere measured: 60.8% of trips in Italy, 63% in France, 53% in Germany, 69% of distance in Switzerland, 68.7% of US workers driving alone. Italy has the highest car ownership and the lowest car occupancy of thirteen EU countries; on work trips in Switzerland 92% of car legs carry one person. Sources: [GR-008](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-008_isfort_2025_rapporto-mobilita-22-sintesi.md), [GR-042](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-042_sdes_2023_mobilite-locale-et-longue-distance-des-francais.md), [GR-041](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-041_bmv-infas_2025_mobilitaet-in-deutschland-2023.md), [GR-035](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-035_bfs-are_2023_mikrozensus-mobilitaet-verkehr-2021.md), [GR-016](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-016_eurostat_2021_passenger-mobility-statistics.md). Confidence: high.
- **F13.** Reliability and frequency make public transport attractive, but improving reliability alone moves demand little, because car users already have reliability and flexibility. Convenience comes first when choosing (42% in Sydney, cost 28%, sustainability 7%); public transport is considered only if it takes less than about double the driving time. The car costs little at the point of use because its fixed costs are already paid. 22% of Europeans find it hard to change habits. Sources: [AP-001](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-001_goransson_2023_factors-that-make-public.md) (PDF p.7, p.11), [GR-037](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-037_imove-australia_2021_sydney-maas-trial-final-report.md) (PDF p.76, p.120), [GR-009](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-009_oecd-itf_2021_integrating-public-transport-into-maas.md) (PDF p.13), [GR-006](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-006_european-commission_2026_swd-300-impact-assessment.md) (PDF p.12). Confidence: medium.
- **F52.** Habits change at life events, not through campaigns. Among Europeans who changed habits in five years the reasons are the cost of transport (32%), a change in the family (30%), moving home (21%) and a new job (16%). Free tickets or road closures change behaviour while they last, and "most people return to car use" afterwards. Stated intentions to use the car less usually do not turn into behaviour. Sources: [GR-017](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-017_ipsos-europ-assistance_2025_mobility-barometer.md) (PDF p.19), [AP-017](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-017_vos_2017_travel-satisfaction-revisited-pivotal.md) (PDF p.13), [AP-001](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-001_goransson_2023_factors-that-make-public.md) (PDF p.8), [GR-008](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-008_isfort_2025_rapporto-mobilita-22-sintesi.md) (PDF p.13–14). Confidence: medium.
- Still open: the criteria Italian intercity commuters name themselves, and in which order.

**Q14. How do people compare options described in different ways?** Partial.

- **F14.** On 100 EU routes, options combining modes existed on 76%, but platforms rarely show or combine them. In the 2024 Eurobarometer (25,805 interviews) 31% had never booked a journey combining modes; among those who rarely or never do, 17% could not find a suitable combination and 5% could not buy all tickets in one place. The Commission's own estimate of the gain is small: at most 1.7% more rail journeys. Source: [GR-006](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-006_european-commission_2026_swd-300-impact-assessment.md) (PDF p.12–13). Confidence: medium.
- **F15.** The rail industry reads the same survey as 61% finding multimodal booking easy. Source: [AR-003](Sources/Articles/AR-003_cer_2025_eurobarometer-press-release.md). Confidence: low (interested party). The two readings differ in emphasis more than in numbers.
- People do not believe planner results they cannot check: a passenger doubts that a trip with three changes of five minutes is faster than the direct one ([OF-002](Sources/Online_Forums/OF-002_eisenbahnforum_2016_eisenbahnforum-reiseerlebnisse-bahn.md)); two phones give different trains for the same search, and a teenager "has no trust in the app's information" ([OR-013](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-013_apple-app-store_2026_sncf-connect-fr.md)). A German reviewer asks for "a convenient way to compare alternatives over several routes" ([OR-007](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-007_apple-app-store_2026_db-navigator-de.md)). Showing the price next to each alternative is valued ([OR-003](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-003_apple-app-store_2026_citymapper-gb.md)). Confidence: low.

**Q15. Where do connections between legs work or break?** Partial, well covered.

- **F16.** A change costs more than its minutes. Walking and waiting weigh about twice the time on board; the pure cost of a change in Madrid was estimated at 15 to 18 minutes of travel time; a change between bus and rail costs about twice one between trains; a guaranteed connection is worth about 9 minutes. Focus groups in Madrid and Vitoria name two costs not tied to time: the mental effort of staying alert, and the interruption of what one was doing ("I take the longest route with the fewest possible transfers, even though it takes longer"). Real-time information makes the wait feel shorter. Sources: [AP-001](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-001_goransson_2023_factors-that-make-public.md) (PDF p.4, p.6), [AP-021](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-021_jimenez_2018_user-perception-transfers-multimodal.md) (PDF p.8–11), [AP-028](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-028_shires_2018_impact-planned-disruptions-rail.md) (PDF p.18). Leg: transfer. Confidence: high (urban studies; the authors warn that changes between cities may differ).
- **F48.** Planners propose the tightest change and regular travellers do not trust it. A Swiss commuter is always shown the bus that leaves 4 to 5 minutes at the station, "the buses are always late in the morning and I have missed the train several times", and now searches bus and train separately. A Trenitalia reviewer asks for "alternative combinations with wider connections". A US reviewer has the opposite problem: the app assumes a planned connection cannot be made. In German travel diaries a 3-minute connection decides an 8-hour trip, and a delay becomes hours when the last train of the evening is missed. Sources: [OR-014](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-014_apple-app-store_2026_sbb-mobile-ch.md) (2026-08-18), [OR-009](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-009_apple-app-store_2026_trenitalia-app-it.md) (2026-08-08), [OR-004](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-004_apple-app-store_2026_transit-us.md), [OF-002](Sources/Online_Forums/OF-002_eisenbahnforum_2016_eisenbahnforum-reiseerlebnisse-bahn.md). Leg: transfer, first mile. Confidence: low (single accounts, though from four countries).
- **F17.** Operators sell connections they do not keep: "combinations of connections that you cannot keep because of delays and trains that do not wait" (Trenitalia); "impossible to make a connection with another bus or a train" (FlixBus); "missed connections … we have no other choice on many routes" (SNCF). Sources: [OR-009](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-009_apple-app-store_2026_trenitalia-app-it.md), [OR-006](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-006_apple-app-store_2026_flixbus-it.md), [OR-013](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-013_apple-app-store_2026_sncf-connect-fr.md). Confidence: low.

**Q16. What trade-offs do people accept, and how much do first and last mile weigh?** Partial.

- Access to public transport is on foot: in Switzerland 68% of walks to a stop are 500 m or less, and car or bike access is rare. People walk about 150 m to a shared bike and up to 500 m to a train. Sources: [GR-035](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-035_bfs-are_2023_mikrozensus-mobilitaet-verkehr-2021.md) (PDF p.39), [GR-012](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-012_uitp_2023_policy-brief-mobility-hubs.md) (PDF p.3). Confidence: medium.
- Help for the first and last mile has had little effect in trials: a 5-dollar incentive for ride-hailing to the station attracted very few people in Sydney; an automated shuttle to a Danish campus did not raise public transport use; shared mobility hubs in European pilots were known by over 60% and little used, and not for commuting. Sources: [GR-037](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-037_imove-australia_2021_sydney-maas-trial-final-report.md), [AP-001](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-001_goransson_2023_factors-that-make-public.md) (PDF p.7), [GR-015](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-015_eumo_2025_shared-mobility-hubs-sharedimobihub.md). Confidence: medium. Operators' own data say the opposite for scooters and bikes ([GR-010](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-010_egum_2024_public-transport-shared-mobility.md)), with an interest.
- A feeder to a trunk line "will not work if that line is unattractive or the trip is less convenient than by car" ([GR-009](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-009_oecd-itf_2021_integrating-public-transport-into-maas.md), PDF p.14). The papers on access and egress ([AP-008](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-008_rahman_2022_first-last-mile-public.md), [AP-010](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-010_azimi_2020_mode-choice-behavior-access.md), [AP-025](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-025_wang_2020_exploring-physical-mental-health.md)) could not be obtained.

### Information during the journey (Q17–Q19)

**Q17. What do commuters need to know on each leg, and when?** Partial.

- At a change: "when I must be on which platform", not a map; on board: where the trip stands and whether the change still holds; at the stop: whether to wait, switch or give up. Information during the trip is rated more important than before it (83% against 65% among rail passengers in an older US survey), and it has value even when nothing can be changed, because it lets people warn others and lowers anxiety. Sources: [OR-014](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-014_apple-app-store_2026_sbb-mobile-ch.md), [OR-015](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-015_apple-app-store_2026_9292-nl.md) (2025-11-23), [GR-040](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-040_trb_2003_tcrp-92-traveler-information.md) (PDF p.18, p.27–29). Leg: transfer, main leg. Confidence: medium.
- Still open: the same for the first and last mile, and for the car.

**Q18. Where is information fragmented, late or inconsistent?** Partial.

- **F18.** Information depends on who owns the data. In 2019 Trenitalia sued a third-party app that showed delays on more than 3 million phones; a forum user wrote that he is "spoilt for choice to know where a plane is" and needs "a crystal ball" for a train. The planner 9292 answers a complaint by saying it depends on what operators send. In Japan one railway group's app cannot search with real-time delays or sell another group's tickets. Sources: [OF-001](Sources/Online_Forums/OF-001_ferrovie-it-forum_2019_ferrovie-it-trenitalia-sues-delay-app.md), [OR-015](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-015_apple-app-store_2026_9292-nl.md) (2026-09-17), [GR-047](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-047_mlit_2025_chiiki-koutsuu-digital-gijutsu-report.md) (PDF p.13–16), [GR-055](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-055_gtfs-de_nd_realtime-data-germany.md). Confidence: medium.
- **F19.** Live status is what regular rail travellers rely on and it often fails: Italo's tracking "almost never works" for a Turin–Milan carnet holder; Trenitalia's "is always wrong" on one line; in Germany a daily user acts "as if the train came on time" whatever the app says. Two apps are needed for one trip when the bus delay disappears from the rail app. Sources: [OR-010](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-010_apple-app-store_2026_italo-it.md), [OR-009](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-009_apple-app-store_2026_trenitalia-app-it.md), [OR-007](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-007_apple-app-store_2026_db-navigator-de.md). Leg: main leg, transfer. Confidence: medium (more than ten independent reviews: a perception).

**Q19. Which sources do people trust?** Weak.

- 45% of British rail passengers have low or no trust in the rail industry, yet most would recommend the train. A French regular asks the operator not to announce a delay "if you are not at least somewhat sure" of its length. Bad underlying data destroys trust in the tool that shows it: "ghost buses", times that drivers themselves call wrong. Sources: [GR-051](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-051_transport-focus_2026_rail-customer-experience-survey.md) (section 11), [OR-013](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-013_apple-app-store_2026_sncf-connect-fr.md), [OF-003](Sources/Online_Forums/OF-003_hacker-news_2023_hn-citymapper-joins-via.md), [OR-004](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-004_apple-app-store_2026_transit-us.md), [GR-040](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-040_trb_2003_tcrp-92-traveler-information.md) (PDF p.110). Confidence: low. Which source a commuter believes when two disagree is still open.

### When plans change (Q20–Q23)

**Q20. Which unexpected situations occur, on which leg?** Partial for rail, open for the rest.

- 17% of British rail passengers said their journey was delayed, 62% of them by less than ten minutes. On an average weekday in Great Britain there are about 39 planned engineering disruptions, most served by replacement bus. In Italy 229 extreme weather events interrupted rail services between 2010 and 2025. In Switzerland 21% of drivers are in a traffic jam on the way to work at least once a week. Sources: [GR-051](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-051_transport-focus_2026_rail-customer-experience-survey.md), [AP-028](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-028_shires_2018_impact-planned-disruptions-rail.md) (table 1), [GR-004](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-004_legambiente_2025_pendolaria-20-edizione.md) (PDF p.24), [GR-035](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-035_bfs-are_2023_mikrozensus-mobilitaet-verkehr-2021.md) (PDF p.37). Confidence: medium. Nothing is broken down by leg, and nothing covers parking or the last mile.

**Q21. How do people find out, and how early?** Partial.

- **F43.** Delay information is late or wrong in both directions. Too late: the delay announced in the minute the train should leave, the platform change two minutes before (Germany); a bus shown on time for three hours and then cancelled (Italy). Wrong the other way: a 40-minute delay announced and then the bus on time; the train in the station before the delay shown has passed. Stale: a notification to go to the platform for a train cancelled six hours earlier; "all green" on the commuter screen while stuck in the train. Too many: 8 to 10 identical notices every morning, so the user turns them off and loses the useful ones. Sources: [OR-007](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-007_apple-app-store_2026_db-navigator-de.md), [OR-006](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-006_apple-app-store_2026_flixbus-it.md), [OR-013](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-013_apple-app-store_2026_sncf-connect-fr.md), [OR-014](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-014_apple-app-store_2026_sbb-mobile-ch.md), [OR-009](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-009_apple-app-store_2026_trenitalia-app-it.md). Leg: main leg. Confidence: medium (many independent reviews in five countries: a perception).
- Knowing about a planned disruption before reaching the station changes behaviour little ([AP-028](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-028_shires_2018_impact-planned-disruptions-rail.md), PDF p.28; non-commuters). In Italy an operator was fined twice for not notifying cancellations and delays to passengers who had left their contact details ([GR-003](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-003_art_2025_relazione-annuale.md), PDF p.143).

**Q22. What do they do next?** Partial.

- **F20.** Among delayed rail passengers in Great Britain, 60% were satisfied with how easy it was to find information, but only 37% with the information on alternative routes, onward travel or connections, and 40% with how clearly the reason was explained. Focus groups say the same: "customers would like timely and honest explanations of delays"; they forgive the fault more easily than the lack of information. Sources: [GR-051](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-051_transport-focus_2026_rail-customer-experience-survey.md) (section 4), [GR-040](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-040_trb_2003_tcrp-92-traveler-information.md) (PDF p.18), [GR-027](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-027_orr_2026_annual-rail-consumer-report-2025-26.md). Leg: main leg, transfer. Confidence: high.
- **F42.** Apps do not carry the traveller through a disruption. "My train was late and as a result I missed the connection but no suggestion by the app for alternatives" (Trainline). Alternatives "are often not feasible as train delays and cancellations are not taken into account"; after tapping an alternative "one no longer has any idea of the current status of the original trip"; after a missed connection the app congratulates on the punctual arrival of the missed train (DB Navigator). One ticket exchanged and all the tickets of a trip with connections are deleted (SNCF). The operator's own page admits that an alternative updates the plan, not the ticket. At a station in Germany staff were "as lost as the passengers", and a late announcement removed the one alternative: "why did he not say so earlier, I could have gone via Gera". Sources: [OR-005](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-005_apple-app-store_2026_trainline-gb.md), [OR-007](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-007_apple-app-store_2026_db-navigator-de.md), [OR-013](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-013_apple-app-store_2026_sncf-connect-fr.md), [PR-007](Sources/Products/PR-007_db-fernverkehr_2026_db-navigator.md), [OF-002](Sources/Online_Forums/OF-002_eisenbahnforum_2016_eisenbahnforum-reiseerlebnisse-bahn.md). Confidence: medium.
- **F21.** What people actually do. Faced with planned works, 66 to 74% took the replacement bus and about 20% left rail; with a diverted train about 9% left. They treat the disruption as a fixed cost more than counting minutes, tolerate a single day and resent a week. Over the following year 10 to 13% of rail trips were lost. In reviews, people buy a second ticket, take a taxi (63, 600 euros) or go back to their first plan. Sources: [AP-028](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-028_shires_2018_impact-planned-disruptions-rail.md) (PDF p.15, p.26–28), [OR-006](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-006_apple-app-store_2026_flixbus-it.md), [OR-015](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-015_apple-app-store_2026_9292-nl.md), [OR-011](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-011_apple-app-store_2026_kakao-t-kr.md). Confidence: medium. The study excluded commuters on purpose. Two simulation papers ([AP-009](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-009_leng_2020_role-information-availability-passengers.md), [AP-011](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-011_drabicki_2023_should-i-stay-or.md)) could not be obtained.
- People put up with it. In focus groups commuting "horror stories" came with the belief that "nothing much can be done about them" ([GR-040](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-040_trb_2003_tcrp-92-traveler-information.md)); a German survey calls transport "shaped by routines".

**Q23. What does a disrupted journey cost?** Partial.

- Satisfaction with the journey falls from 92% with no delay to 69% for 5 to 14 minutes and 40% for 30 minutes or more. A stressful commute may lower performance and satisfaction at work. Three in ten people in five Italian cities gave up work, study or care opportunities because of travel difficulties. Compensation exists and is hard to get: 8.3 million claims a year in Great Britain; in Italy season-ticket holders of two operators were not being paid; reviewers in three countries ask for it to be automatic. Sources: [GR-051](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-051_transport-focus_2026_rail-customer-experience-survey.md), [AP-017](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-017_vos_2017_travel-satisfaction-revisited-pivotal.md) (PDF p.5), [GR-004](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-004_legambiente_2025_pendolaria-20-edizione.md) (PDF p.50), [GR-053](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-053_orr_2025_complaints-delay-compensation-2024-25.md), [GR-003](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-003_art_2025_relazione-annuale.md) (PDF p.142). Confidence: medium.

### Current tools and workarounds (Q24–Q26)

**Q24. Which tools do commuters use?** Partial.

- **F22.** The 17 products fall into four kinds: general planners, ticket sellers, operators' apps and broader mobility apps. Operators' apps now claim the whole trip: DB Navigator, NS, SNCF Connect and SBB Mobile plan and sell urban transport, show shared bikes and name commuters; SBB claims to accompany the traveller "from door to door". The Italian apps claim less: Trenitalia's page mentions the daily trip once, Italo's speaks to tourists, and Telepass joins car, parking, trains and urban tickets only as payments. Sources: [PR-001](Sources/Products/PR-001_google_2026_google-maps.md) to [PR-017](Sources/Products/PR-017_bodlaender_2026_sprinter.md). Confidence: low (vendors' own pages, nothing tested).
- What people use in fact: among British rail passengers who looked for information, 68% used an app and 18% a general map; 63% of app ticket buyers used the reseller and 20% the operator. In Sydney the preferred tools were the general map and a simple timetable app that saves regular trips; the integrated app came third. Sources: [GR-051](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-051_transport-focus_2026_rail-customer-experience-survey.md), [GR-037](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-037_imove-australia_2021_sydney-maas-trial-final-report.md) (PDF p.122). Confidence: medium.
- Still open: which tools an Italian intercity commuter uses on each leg.

**Q25. How do people move between tools?** Weak. A Sydney participant: "App swapping is annoying … checking Google Maps then making sure I have my Opal card then … ordering an Uber because I became late through so many apps" ([AP-014](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-014_smith_2023_mobility-as-service-users.md)). Reviewers check a second app or website when the first fails: the stations' websites for Italo, a regional app for SBB, another seller for Trenitalia. In Italy "every municipality has its own parking app" ([OR-012](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-012_apple-app-store_2026_telepass-it.md)). The Korean ministry describes the normal case as checking the route and then booking each means in its own app ([GR-049](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-049_molit-korea_2023_k-maas-pilot-announcement.md)). Confidence: low.

**Q26. What workarounds have people developed?** Weak, no longer open. Taking an earlier train or bus than the planner proposes; searching each leg separately; acting as if the train were on time whatever the delay shown; choosing the route with fewest changes; keeping a ticket on paper; splitting tickets by hand; following a companion who knows the station. Sources: [GR-004](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-004_legambiente_2025_pendolaria-20-edizione.md), [OR-014](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-014_apple-app-store_2026_sbb-mobile-ch.md), [OR-007](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-007_apple-app-store_2026_db-navigator-de.md), [AP-021](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-021_jimenez_2018_user-perception-transfers-multimodal.md), [OR-005](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-005_apple-app-store_2026_trainline-gb.md). Confidence: low. Italian commuter groups, where most of this would be found, could not be read.

### Why now (Q27–Q33)

**Q27. Why do the frictions persist?** Partial, see F25 to F31, F49 and F50.

**Q28. What do current tools do well, and where do they stop?** Partial.

- **F23.** In 1,555 recent App Store reviews of the 17 apps, 53% gave one or two stars. A keyword coding of those found tickets, payment and refunds first (27%), then bugs (15%), real-time information (11%), prices (11%) and a recent redesign (10%). The reviews have now all been read, and the reading by theme for each app is in the source files. It confirms the order and adds what the keywords missed: most anger follows an update; apps are judged for the service behind them (F47); regular users want less on the screen (F45). Sources: [OR-001](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-001_apple-app-store_2026_google-maps-it.md) to [OR-017](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-017_apple-app-store_2026_sprinter-nl.md). Confidence: low. Recent written reviews are far more negative than the whole user base.
- **F24.** What tools do well: buying a simple ticket; making an unknown city usable; showing the vehicle approaching. Where they stop: at the change, in a disruption, at the edge of the operator's own network, and at the door ("we'll get you to the door" gave a three-hour walk). Sources: [OR-003](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-003_apple-app-store_2026_citymapper-gb.md), [OR-005](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-005_apple-app-store_2026_trainline-gb.md), [OF-003](Sources/Online_Forums/OF-003_hacker-news_2023_hn-citymapper-joins-via.md). Confidence: low.
- **F47.** Apps are blamed for the service. "If the app … does not tell me the constant delays of the regional trains, the traveller can no longer rely even on the app" (Moovit, Milan); "the app is quick but the trains are not" (NS); "in 7 days of commuting, maybe twice they are on time" (Trenitalia). The reverse also holds for Italo: "how can such a good service have such an embarrassing app". Sources: [OR-002](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-002_apple-app-store_2026_moovit-it.md), [OR-008](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-008_apple-app-store_2026_ns-app-nl.md), [OR-009](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-009_apple-app-store_2026_trenitalia-app-it.md), [OR-010](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-010_apple-app-store_2026_italo-it.md), [OR-004](Sources/Online_Reviews/OR-004_apple-app-store_2026_transit-us.md). Confidence: medium.
- Commercial content gets in the way at the urgent moment: a compulsory advert that made a user miss a bus (Moovit); insurance and car hire offers that hide the trip (SNCF); marketing notices that push users to switch all notifications off (Trainline, Trenitalia). Confidence: medium (many reviews).

**Q29. Missing information, or information that exists but is scattered or untrusted?** Partial. The information on the normal trip exists and is easy to find (95% of British passengers found planning information easily). What is missing is specific: what to do next in a disruption (F20, F42), a delay estimate one can act on (F43), and one view across operators (F18). Trust is damaged by wrong data more than by scattered data.

**Q30. What prevents integration between services?** Partial, well covered.

- **F25.** Since 2017 the EU requires a national access point for travel data; real-time data were optional until the 2024 amendment. Italy's access point collects static data from 16 regions, Trenitalia, Italo and FlixBus; four regions are missing. In May 2026 the Commission proposed rules on rail ticketing and multimodal booking. Sources: [GR-021](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-021_european-union_2017_delegated-regulation-2017-1926.md), [GR-054](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-054_cciss_nd_italian-national-access-point-mmtis.md), [GR-006](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-006_european-commission_2026_swd-300-impact-assessment.md), [AR-001](Sources/Articles/AR-001_railway-gazette_2026_passenger-package.md). Confidence: high.
- **F50.** The new rules stop at the city limits. The proposed regulation does not apply to services whose main purpose is to serve an urban centre or to connect it with the surrounding area, so the first and last legs of a commute stay outside the single ticket and its rights. Planners that inform without selling, and operators' own apps, are also outside. Two tickets for one journey mean no rights when a connection is missed. In Italy fare systems do not even carry over between train categories: "if a high-speed train is late a traveller cannot choose to board an Intercity or a regional train". Sources: [GR-007](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-007_european-commission_2026_com-231-multimodal-booking.md) (Article 2, PDF p.15), [GR-006](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-006_european-commission_2026_swd-300-impact-assessment.md) (PDF p.6, p.14), [GR-004](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-004_legambiente_2025_pendolaria-20-edizione.md). Leg: first and last mile, transfer. Confidence: high.
- **F26.** Commercial interest is a barrier. Incumbents' own channels hold about 80 to 90% of online long-distance rail sales in Germany, France and Spain; a German court forbade the operator's app from presetting a filter that favoured its own trains; operators fear losing the customer to a "gatekeeper"; the railways' association calls the 2026 proposals "unjustified regulatory interventionism". Sources: [GR-006](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-006_european-commission_2026_swd-300-impact-assessment.md) (PDF p.16, p.18), [GR-009](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-009_oecd-itf_2021_integrating-public-transport-into-maas.md) (PDF p.27), [AR-001](Sources/Articles/AR-001_railway-gazette_2026_passenger-package.md). Confidence: high.
- Where it works, one body owns the data or the fare: the Dutch planner belongs to the operators and an independent developer can build on the open data; in Switzerland one pass covers the country; Norway has one trip-planning engine for the whole country; Finland obliged operators by law to open ticketing. Sources: [PR-015](Sources/Products/PR-015_reisinformatiegroep_2026_9292.md), [PR-017](Sources/Products/PR-017_bodlaender_2026_sprinter.md), [PR-014](Sources/Products/PR-014_sbb_2026_sbb-mobile.md), [OF-004](Sources/Online_Forums/OF-004_hacker-news_2025_hn-i-built-my-own-citymapper.md), [GR-039](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-039_nordic-policy-centre_2022_finland-maas-legislation.md). Confidence: medium. Even there the complaints about tight changes and late feeder buses remain (F48).

**Q31. How well do tools reflect personal preferences and context?** Weak. Passes and discount cards are not kept or not counted, so people pay for what they already hold (SNCF, SBB, NS, Trainline). Users object when the app decides for them: changing the route without asking (Google Maps), assigning a taxi 20 minutes away (Kakao T, with the proposal "ask first"). Confidence: low.

**Q32. What has changed recently?** Partial, the best covered question. Hybrid work and its link with distance (F3, F38 to F41); the EU package of May 2026 (F25, F50); the Italian MaaS programme (F49); flexible passes; Germany's flat monthly ticket, now the usual ticket of 58% of daily public transport users ([GR-041](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-041_bmv-infas_2025_mobilitaet-in-deutschland-2023.md), p.106–109); a new commuting matrix for Italy, 2021, published in October 2025 and not yet opened ([GR-026](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-026_istat_2025_matrici-contiguita-distanza-pendolarismo.md)).

**Q33. What can be learned from integrated services so far?** Partial, well covered.

- **F27.** In Japan about 43% of MaaS projects from 2019 to 2024 ended as trials; the ministry says one-stop access "has not been achieved" because each railway group builds its own app. Source: [GR-047](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-047_mlit_2025_chiiki-koutsuu-digital-gijutsu-report.md) (PDF p.13–17). Confidence: medium.
- **F28.** MaaS Global (Whim) raised over 149 million euros, had about 10,000 monthly users in Helsinki at its peak and went bankrupt in 2024. Citymapper, with tens of millions of users, was sold at a loss: "consumer-facing routing is a commodity". A Dutch institute sums up the first decade as "the perceived problem was a tech issue, and the solution was an app". Sources: [AR-006](Sources/Articles/AR-006_frost-sullivan_2024_maas-global-collapse.md), [AR-005](Sources/Articles/AR-005_techcrunch_2023_via-acquires-citymapper.md), [OF-003](Sources/Online_Forums/OF-003_hacker-news_2023_hn-citymapper-joins-via.md), [AR-010](Sources/Articles/AR-010_global-fleet_2024_maas-1-is-dead.md). Confidence: medium.
- **F29.** The Sydney trial: 99% of trips without the app; the planner hardly used, "partly due to habit"; what participants valued was the personal onboarding, the single monthly bill ("Do I really spend this much on my travel?") and the discounts. "Much more than an app and a set of subscription plans is needed." Sources: [GR-037](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-037_imove-australia_2021_sydney-maas-trial-final-report.md), [AP-014](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-014_smith_2023_mobility-as-service-users.md). Confidence: medium (one trial, two reports).
- **F30.** A round table of public authorities and operators: the effect of MaaS on public transport demand "remains to be demonstrated at scale"; some argued that "international and intercity seamlessness is of little importance among the user base"; pilots should last at least six months, because people do not rearrange their lives around an offer they expect to end. Source: [GR-009](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-009_oecd-itf_2021_integrating-public-transport-into-maas.md) (PDF p.6, p.14, p.16). Confidence: medium. A Swedish trial where 42% reported a change of behaviour is known only at second hand ([AP-001](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-001_goransson_2023_factors-that-make-public.md), PDF p.10).
- **F49.** Italy's national programme cost 56.9 million euros and by May 2026 counted almost 950,000 trips, over 16 per user, which implies about 59,000 users (my calculation). ISFORT says the response of demand to digital innovation "is still weak". Sources: [GR-050](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-050_mit_2026_maas-for-italy-white-paper.md), [GR-008](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-008_isfort_2025_rapporto-mobilita-22-sintesi.md) (PDF p.48). Confidence: medium. The white paper itself was not downloaded.
- **F31.** What users may want first is a service that works: "presumably no travel app will improve the actual services" ([AP-016](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-016_mcilroy_2024_reservation-i-have-that.md), title and abstract only). Now supported by F46 and F47. Confidence: medium.

### What success (Q34–Q40)

**Q34, Q35, Q39. What makes a journey go well, and how would improvement be noticed?** Partial.

- **F46.** Punctuality first. Asked what would make them use public transport more, Italians answer punctuality (41%), then cost (38%) and availability (30%); in the rest of Europe cost comes first. In Germany the obstacles are no or poor connections (more than half the population), travel time (40%) and unreliability (40%), far ahead of unclear fares (15%) and too little information (16%). US riders name frequency and accurate arrival times ahead of fares. Sources: [GR-017](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-017_ipsos-europ-assistance_2025_mobility-barometer.md) (PDF p.20, chart image), [GR-041](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-041_bmv-infas_2025_mobilitaet-in-deutschland-2023.md) (p.104–106), [GR-018](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-018_moovit_2024_global-public-transport-report.md). Confidence: high.
- **F32.** Predictability is what users ask of the journey: "I would rather longer waits and better predictability"; travellers "seek to lower the trip time uncertainty" and want "control over time and travel decisions". Longer trips are less satisfying, and passengers on longer distances are less satisfied. ISTAT titled a section "the range of trips and the uncertainty of their duration are growing". Sources: [OF-004](Sources/Online_Forums/OF-004_hacker-news_2025_hn-i-built-my-own-citymapper.md), [GR-040](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-040_trb_2003_tcrp-92-traveler-information.md) (PDF p.22), [AP-017](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-017_vos_2017_travel-satisfaction-revisited-pivotal.md), [AP-001](Sources/Academic_Papers/AP-001_goransson_2023_factors-that-make-public.md) (PDF p.8), [GR-024](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-024_istat_2018_spostamenti-quotidiani-e-nuove-forme-di-mobilita.md). Confidence: medium.
- Time on board is used and valued by some: rail travellers work or study, and avoid changes to protect it; others rate the possibility low. Confidence: low.

**Q36. Planning a multimodal trip with confidence?** Weak. Signs: wider connections on request (F48), a guaranteed connection (F16), a guaranteed way home ([GR-009](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-009_oecd-itf_2021_integrating-public-transport-into-maas.md)), and availability one can count on at the hub ([GR-012](Sources/Global_Reports/GR-012_uitp_2023_policy-brief-mobility-hubs.md)).

**Q37. Handling a disruption calmly?** Partial. What the evidence points to: the reason, stated honestly; a delay estimate that is not announced unless it is fairly sure; alternatives that take the live situation into account; the current trip kept in view; a ticket that follows the new route; compensation without a claim (F20, F42, F43).

**Q38. Which decisions do commuters want to keep?** Weak. Users object to tools that decide for them (Q31) and in Sydney to committing a month in advance (F41). Nothing direct.

**Q40. Which problems are worth investigating further?** Not answered here: it is the outcome of the synthesis and Marco's choice. The evidence points most strongly at four areas: the disrupted journey (F20, F42, F43); the hybrid week and what to buy for it (F38 to F41); the change between legs and its buffer (F16, F48); and the daily check of a known trip (F45).

## Decisions taken

- **The file keeps its name and its finding numbers**, so that links from the interview script still work. New findings start at F33.
- **Confidence** follows the rules in the instructions with the grades in the source files, which do not yet count citations among sources.
- **Reviews** are reported as perceptions. A finding that rests on many independent reviews saying the same thing is rated medium, as the rules allow; one that rests on single reviews is rated low.
- **Sources outside the scope** in context (Japanese rural areas, Korean taxis, US city buses) are used only for mechanisms that transfer, and this is said where they are used.
- **Papers known only from the abstract** are used with that label and never as the only support of a finding rated above low.
