# Initial Project Questions

Open-ended questions for the discovery phase, based on the project brief in `brief.md`. They are meant to guide desk research and user interviews, not to point to a solution.

## Who

1. Who makes intercity commutes on a regular or semi-regular basis, and what are their reasons for travelling (work, study, care, other)?
2. How do commuters differ by travel frequency, and how does a daily commuter's experience compare with that of someone who travels once or twice a week?
3. How does familiarity with a route change the way people plan and what they pay attention to?
4. What constraints shape each person's journey: fixed arrival times, cost, family duties, accessibility needs, luggage, physical effort?
5. Which mobility options does each commuter actually have available at each end of the trip, and which are out of reach for them and why?
6. Which commuters regularly combine several modes in one journey, and which stick to a single mode? What explains the difference?
7. Who else is affected by or involved in the commuter's travel decisions (employer, family, travel companions)?
8. Which groups within the broader commuter population experience the most friction, and which are least served by existing services?

## What

### Planning before leaving

9. How do people decide how to travel on a given day, and when do they make that decision?
10. What does planning look like for a routine trip compared with an unusual one (new destination, different time, strike, bad weather)?
11. What information do people look for before leaving, and where do they find it?
12. Which parts of the journey do people plan carefully, and which do they leave to chance?

### Comparing and combining options

13. How do commuters choose between public transport, a private vehicle and other mobility services? What criteria matter most: time, cost, reliability, comfort, flexibility, habit?
14. How do people compare options that are described in different ways, by different services?
15. Where do the connections between modes work well, and where do they break down (parking, first and last mile, ticketing, waiting times)?
16. What trade-offs do people accept, and what do they give up, when they combine modes?

### Information during the journey

17. What do commuters need to know while travelling, and at which moments?
18. Where is information fragmented, unclear, inconsistent, late or hard to compare?
19. Which sources do people trust, which do they distrust, and what shaped that judgement?

### When plans change

20. What unexpected situations occur most often, and which have the heaviest consequences?
21. How do people find out that something has gone wrong, and how early?
22. What do they do next? How do they identify alternatives and choose between them under time pressure?
23. What is the emotional and practical cost of a disrupted journey, for the commuter and for others?

### Current tools and workarounds

24. Which apps, websites, services and informal channels (group chats, colleagues, station staff) do commuters use today?
25. How do they move between these tools during one journey, and what gets lost in the switch?
26. What workarounds, habits or personal rules have people developed to protect themselves from uncertainty?

## Why now

27. Why do these frictions persist despite the number of transport, navigation and mobility services already available?
28. What do current tools do well, and what do they fail to support?
29. Is the problem mainly one of missing information, or of information that exists but is scattered, badly timed, untrusted or not relevant to the person's context?
30. What prevents integration between services: separate operators, data availability, ticketing and payment systems, business models, regulation?
31. How well do existing tools reflect personal preferences, constraints and the specific context of an intercity commute?
32. What has changed recently in commuting patterns, mobility offers or user expectations that makes the problem more or less pressing?
33. What attempts at integrated or multimodal services already exist, and what can be learned from how they have been adopted or abandoned?

## What success

34. How would a commuter describe a journey that went well? What made it feel that way?
35. In which specific situations would reduced friction, uncertainty or mental effort matter most to them?
36. What would it look like for a commuter to plan a multimodal trip with confidence, and what would they no longer have to do?
37. What would it look like for a commuter to handle a disruption calmly, and what would they need at that moment?
38. Which decisions do commuters want to make themselves, and which would they gladly hand over?
39. How would commuters notice that their experience has improved: time saved, fewer missed connections, less stress, lower cost, more predictability?
40. Which of the problems identified are frequent and serious enough to be worth investigating further, and how will we tell?
